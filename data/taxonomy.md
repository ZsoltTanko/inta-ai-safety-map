# Taxonomy for the int/a AI-safety map (v1)

Use ONLY the keys below in `tags`. The profile field name for each facet is given in brackets.

## Tradition (home + secondary)  [tags.tradition; list]
Question: Which body of knowledge, practice or tradition does it bring to making AI go well? First value = home (exactly one); then 0-2 traditions it also draws on substantially (3 for hubs, up to 4 for generalist funders).

- `formal` **Formal & physical sciences**: Wheel 1 (Sciences & engineering). Mathematics, theoretical physics and CS, logic, decision and game theory as foundations for understanding and aligning AI: singular learning theory, computational mechanics, category theory and verification, algorithmic information theory, agent foundations, game theory of cooperation, bargaining and races. Boundary: Complexity, cybernetics and agent-based models go to Life & systems (B6); safety cases to Safety; game theory of markets or power to Economics. Examples: iliad, resolution, mathematical-ai-safety-institute, topos-institute, cooperative-ai-foundation.
- `life` **Life & systems sciences**: Wheel 2 (Sciences & engineering). Sciences of living and self-organising wholes: evolutionary and developmental biology, basal and collective intelligence, artificial life; neuroscience, NeuroAI, active inference, brain emulation; complexity; cybernetics, systems science, ecology, resilience; the systems strand of metacrisis thinking. Boundary: Experience or moral status of AI goes to AI minds (B4); experiments on models to Psychology (B5); proofs to Formal (B6); metamodern work to Contemplative (B7). Examples: principles-of-intelligence, alignment-of-complex-systems-research-group, softmax, levin-lab-allen-discovery-center-at-tufts-univer, astera-institute.
- `minds` **Consciousness & AI minds**: Wheel 3 (Minds & inner life). Consciousness science, sentience science and philosophy of mind applied to whether AI (and animals) have experience, welfare or moral status: indicators, welfare assessments, digital-minds field-building and governance, animal sentience bridged to AI, relational study of model minds. Boundary: Clinical work on mind attribution goes to Psychology (B3); ethics with AI welfare as one strand to Philosophy (B13); AI-rights law to Law; Buddhist accounts of mind to Contemplative. Examples: eleos-ai-research, rethink-priorities, sussex-centre-for-consciousness-science, nyu-center-for-mind-ethics-and-policy, partnership-for-research-into-sentient-machines.
- `psych` **Psychology & cognitive science**: Wheel 4 (Minds & inner life). Clinical psychology, psychiatry, psychoanalysis and peer support; child development; social, moral and wisdom psychology; relationships and affect; computational cognitive science and machine psychology; and coaching and applied-rationality services for AI-safety workers. Boundary: Neuroscience goes to Life & systems (B5); meditation and compassion practice to Contemplative (B2); children's-rights law to Law (B9); AI consciousness to AI minds (B3). Examples: ai-psychological-research-coalition, stanford-brainstorm, common-sense-media, uk-ai-security-institute, american-psychoanalytic-association.
- `contemplative` **Contemplative & integral traditions**: Wheel 5 (Minds & inner life). First-person, practice-based traditions and their science: meditation and contemplative training, contemplative and compassion science, contemplative insight turned into AI design or alignment targets, and the integral, metamodern and inner-development milieu int/a comes from. Boundary: Bodies speaking for a religious tradition, incl. Buddhist federations, translation, media and funders, go to Religion (B1); empirical wellbeing research and field-health services to Psychology (B2). Examples: integral-altruism, flourishing-intelligence-program-centre-for-euda, monastic-academy-for-the-preservation-of-life-on, mind-life-institute, perspectiva.
- `religion` **Religion & theology**: Wheel 6 (Minds & inner life). Organisations that speak from, for or to a religious tradition: churches, denominations, federations, interfaith bodies; theology, religious studies and religious law; faith media, investors, benchmarks and advocacy; chaplaincy; faith and science-and-religion funders. Boundary: Contemplative practice or AI artefacts built from contemplative insight go to Contemplative (B1); religious-founded peace research to Peace (B11); ADL to Law. Examples: ai-and-faith, world-council-of-churches, holy-see, international-islamic-fiqh-academy, consortium-for-evaluating-faith-and-ethics-in-ai.
- `philosophy` **Philosophy & ethics**: Wheel 7 (Humanities & cultures). Moral and political philosophy (longtermist, suffering-focused, virtue, care ethics), philosophy of technology and intelligence, continental and process thought, Chinese and African academic philosophy, applied ethics, bioethics and ethics councils. Boundary: Philosophy of mind mainly about AI consciousness goes to AI minds (B13); theology to Religion; metamodern philosophy to Contemplative. Examples: mint-lab, forethought, institute-for-ethics-in-ai-university-of-oxford, centre-for-technomoral-futures-university-of-edi, meaning-alignment-institute.
- `arts` **Arts, narrative & futures**: Wheel 8 (Humanities & cultures). Imaginative and anticipatory disciplines: art, design and speculative design; narrative, literary and cultural studies; film, games, journalism and communications; futures studies and foresight (scenarios, Delphi, horizon scanning, wargames, technology assessment), incl. stories as model priors. Boundary: STS goes to Law; decolonial speculative work to Indigenous; probabilistic forecasting to Democracy (B10); a community's publication to that community (H3). Examples: ai-futures-project, intelligence-rising, serpentine-arts-technologies, center-for-digital-narrative-and-erc-project-ai, the-millennium-project.
- `indigenous` **Indigenous & decolonial knowledges**: Wheel 9 (Humanities & cultures). Indigenous knowledge systems and data sovereignty (kinship ontologies, tikanga and kaitiakitanga, CARE principles, TK labels, Ubuntu-guided research) and decolonial, Black and feminist standpoint traditions that rebuild AI from marginalised communities' knowledge. Boundary: Knowledge tradition, not geography (B8): Global South or feminist groups working through law, policy, economics or civic tech go to those sectors; location shows in region and standpoint. Examples: abundant-intelligences, te-hiku-media, global-indigenous-data-alliance, te-mana-raraunga-m-ori-data-sovereignty-network, masakhane.
- `law` **Law, rights & society**: Wheel 10 (Society & institutions). Law and legal theory (liability, human, children's and digital rights, whistleblowing, AI-rights advocacy), STS, sociology, anthropology, critical data studies, civil-society auditing, and public-interest, ELSI and development research and funding. Boundary: Labour, competition and political economy go to Economics (B9); deliberation to Democracy; humanitarian law to Peace; certification to Safety. Examples: normativity-lab, tech-justice-law-project, 5rights-foundation, data-society-research-institute, schwartz-reisman-institute-for-technology-and-so.
- `economics` **Economics, labour & commons**: Wheel 11 (Society & institutions). Economics of transformative AI (growth, labour, public finance, cash transfers), political economy and antitrust (power concentration, lock-in), commons, cooperatives and public-goods governance, and organised labour, incl. data workers. Boundary: Game theory of AI agents goes to Formal; process design to Democracy; workers' rights as human rights to Law; complex-systems disempowerment work to Life & systems. Examples: economics-of-transformative-ai-initiative-univer, formation-research, mit-stone-center-on-inequality-and-shaping-the-f, ostrom-workshop, united-tech-and-allied-workers-with-unite-the-un.
- `democracy` **Democracy & collective intelligence**: Wheel 12 (Society & institutions). Deliberative and participatory democracy (assemblies, deliberative polling, sortition, broad listening), collective-intelligence science and AI-for-democracy tools, and collective epistemics: forecasting, argument mapping, fact-checking, provenance, knowledge commons. Boundary: Peacebuilders' deliberative tools go to Peace (B11); mechanism design to Economics; foresight to Arts (B10); rights advocacy to Law. Examples: collective-intelligence-project, computational-democracy-project, stanford-deliberative-democracy-lab, democracynext, vtaiwan-g0v.
- `peace` **Peace, security & diplomacy**: Wheel 13 (Society & institutions). Nuclear risk and arms control, security studies and humanitarian law, Track II, science and multilateral diplomacy (incl. China studies), and peacebuilding, mediation and conflict transformation applied to AI and the AI race. Boundary: Biosecurity and catastrophe resilience go to Safety (B12); interfaith ethics statements to Religion; information integrity to Democracy unless framed as security. Examples: safe-ai-forum, concordia-ai, pugwash-conferences-on-science-and-world-affairs, stockholm-international-peace-research-institute, centre-for-humanitarian-dialogue.
- `safety` **Safety & risk sciences**: Wheel 14 (Sciences & engineering), closing on Formal via verification. Safety-critical engineering and assurance (STAMP/STPA, safety cases, certification, standards), human factors, resilience engineering, incident reporting, risk management, insurance, infosec, catastrophe resilience, public health and biosecurity. Boundary: Verification as mathematics goes to Formal; nonproliferation diplomacy to Peace (B12); rights auditing to Law. Examples: centre-for-assuring-autonomy-university-of-york, mit-partnership-for-systems-approaches-to-safety, ai-incident-database, saferai, artificial-intelligence-underwriting-company.

## Sub-tradition  [tags.sub_tradition; single value]
Question: Within its home tradition, which recognisable school or community is it part of?

- `formal_maths` **Mathematics of learning**: Mathematics, statistical physics, computational mechanics and category theory that explain learning or verify systems. Boundary: Theories of agents and choice go to Agents & game theory. Examples: resolution, mathematical-ai-safety-institute.
- `formal_agents` **Agents & game theory**: Decision theory, algorithmic information theory, causal incentives, and game theory of cooperation, bargaining and races. Boundary: Evolutionary dynamics of living populations go to Biology. Examples: iliad, dovetail.
- `life_biology` **Biology & diverse intelligence**: Developmental and evolutionary biology, basal and animal cognition, collective behaviour, cultural evolution, artificial life. Boundary: Neural systems go to Neuroscience & NeuroAI. Examples: softmax, levin-lab-allen-discovery-center-at-tufts-univer.
- `life_neuro` **Neuroscience & NeuroAI**: Systems and computational neuroscience, active inference, brain-inspired AGI, connectomics and whole-brain emulation. Boundary: Consciousness goes to AI minds; experiments on models to Psychology. Examples: ae-studio, astera-institute.
- `life_complexity` **Complexity & emergence**: Complexity science, information-theoretic emergence, network science and agent-based models of interacting agents. Boundary: Feedback modelling and ecology go to Cybernetics. Examples: santa-fe-institute, complexity-science-hub-vienna.
- `life_cybernetics` **Cybernetics, systems & ecology**: Cybernetics, systems theory, system dynamics, ecology and resilience science, and systems diagnoses of the metacrisis. Boundary: Metamodern and inner-development work goes to Contemplative. Examples: american-society-for-cybernetics, anu-school-of-cybernetics.
- `minds_science` **Consciousness & sentience science**: Theories, indicators and measurement of consciousness and sentience, philosophy of machine consciousness, animal sentience. Boundary: Welfare assessment and field-building go to AI welfare & status. Examples: sussex-centre-for-consciousness-science, association-for-mathematical-consciousness-scien.
- `minds_welfare` **AI welfare & status**: AI welfare and moral-status research, model welfare assessments, public attitudes, readiness and the field's funders and networks. Boundary: Relational close study of models goes to Relational study of minds. Examples: eleos-ai-research, rethink-priorities.
- `minds_relational` **Relational study of minds**: Studying language-model minds through sustained interaction, simulator theory, experimental writing and close reading. Boundary: Buddhist, Indigenous or phenomenological relational accounts keep those homes. Examples: anima-labs, cyborgism-community-and-cyborgism-wiki.
- `psych_clinical` **Clinical psychology & psychiatry**: Psychiatry, psychotherapy, psychoanalysis, crisis practice and lived-experience peer support for AI-related harm. Boundary: Support for AI-safety workers goes to Field health. Examples: stanford-brainstorm, american-psychiatric-association.
- `psych_development` **Child & youth development**: Developmental psychology, paediatrics and youth work on how children and teens use AI, including ratings and funders. Boundary: Children's-rights law goes to Law. Examples: common-sense-media, everyone-ai.
- `psych_social` **Social & wisdom psychology**: Behavioural, social and moral psychology, persuasion, judgement, and the empirical science of wisdom and flourishing. Boundary: Contemplative wisdom practice goes to Contemplative. Examples: center-for-humans-and-machines-max-planck-instit, uk-ai-security-institute.

- `psych_cognitive` **Cognitive & machine psychology**: Computational cognitive science, representational alignment, human-machine teaming and psychology experiments on models. Boundary: Neuroscience goes to Life & systems. Examples: re-align, bramley-computational-cognitive-science-lab-with.

Boundary: Brain-inspired AI without contemplative basis goes to NeuroAI. Examples: flourishing-intelligence-program-centre-for-euda, mind-life-institute.

## Ring of proximity  [tags.ring; single value]
Question: How close is it to the AI-safety core?

- `core` **AI-safety core**: Making advanced AI go well (alignment, safety, catastrophic or existential risk, AI welfare, governance of transformative AI) is the organisation's founding or primary mission; it belongs to the AI-safety and x-risk ecosystem (funded by, staffed from or listed by that community, often on the aisafety.com map); and it imports knowledge or methods from outside mainstream ML alignment and conventional AI policy. Includes AI-safety programmes inside frontier labs and national AI-safety institutes. Boundary: Being on the aisafety.com map is a separate facet. An older institution from another field that opened an AI-safety programme is bridge however large the programme (SIPRI, York CfAA). A new organisation founded by people from another field whose founding mission is AI safety is core (Simplex, Softmax, Eleos). Examples: alignment-of-complex-systems-research-group, principles-of-intelligence, eleos-ai-research.
- `bridge` **Bridge from elsewhere**: Rooted in another field or tradition (its people, institution, funding or identity belong there) and running a sustained, explicit programme on AI safety, catastrophic or existential risk, alignment, superintelligence, AI welfare or 'AI going well'. Sustained means a standing programme, funded project, fellowship, publication line or repeated convening in 2024-26, not a single event. Boundary: AI work on present harms with no safety, catastrophic-risk, alignment or 'going well' framing is adjacent. A single talk, essay or workshop is potential. Examples: amaranth-foundation, ai-and-faith, the-millennium-project.
- `adjacent` **Adjacent, no x-risk frame**: Has a sustained AI programme rooted in another tradition, focused on AI's human, societal or cultural effects (harms, rights, wellbeing, labour, meaning, democracy, religion), without catastrophic-risk, alignment or 'AI going well' framing. Boundary: If it starts framing its work around catastrophic risk, alignment or AI welfare, it moves to bridge. If its AI engagement is light (events, essays, one study), it is potential. Examples: data-society-research-institute, stop-killer-robots, american-psychological-association.
- `potential` **Potential partner**: Holds highly relevant knowledge, methods or reach, but its AI engagement is light (occasional events, essays, a single paper or session) or absent. The profiler must name, in one line, the transferable method that justifies inclusion. Boundary: Once it runs a standing AI programme it moves to adjacent or bridge. Empty or thin potential cells may be search artefacts (English-only search, exhausted search budgets, no sweep of statistics, operations research or logic) and are labelled as such on the map. Examples: ostrom-workshop, garrison-institute, resilience-engineering-association.

## Problem addressed  [tags.problems; list]
Question: Which parts of 'making the transition to advanced AI go well' does its work bear on? (1-3 values; the first is the primary problem.)

- `agency` **Understanding AI & agency**: [The AI itself] A scientific or formal account of what AI systems are and do: how goals, agency, learning, world-models and capabilities arise, interpretability from physics, neuroscience, biology or cognitive science, and how agents could be designed differently. Boundary: Moral status, values, testing deployed systems and many-agent dynamics have their own columns. Examples: principles-of-intelligence, simplex, levin-lab-allen-discovery-center-at-tufts-univer.
- `values` **What AI should value**: [The AI itself] The alignment target: which values, virtues, wisdom, care or character AI should have, and how to specify, train or evaluate for them from moral, contemplative, religious, Indigenous, developmental or narrative traditions. Boundary: Addressing people about dignity, not models, is Meaning; add Democratic voice when public input yields a model spec. Examples: meaning-alignment-institute, flourishing-intelligence-program-centre-for-euda, positive-ai-labs.
- `assurance` **Assurance & oversight**: [The AI itself] Making specific AI systems demonstrably safe and overseeable: evals and red-teaming, verification and safety cases, certification and standards, audits, incident reporting, human factors of oversight, insurance of deployed systems. Boundary: Internals go to Agency; liability to Rights; misuse by states to Security; recovery to Systemic risk. Examples: centre-for-assuring-autonomy-university-of-york, mit-partnership-for-systems-approaches-to-safety, ai-incident-database.
- `ai_status` **Moral status of AI**: [The AI itself] Whether AI systems are or could be subjects whose experiences matter, and what relationship follows: consciousness indicators, welfare assessments, digital-minds governance, relational study of model minds, AI-rights advocacy. Boundary: Harm to humans from mind attribution is Protecting minds (code both if both). Examples: eleos-ai-research, rethink-priorities, nyu-center-for-mind-ethics-and-policy.
- `minds` **Protecting minds & relationships**: [Human minds & meaning] What AI does to human minds, development and relationships: companion harms, AI-associated psychosis and dependency, children's development, attention and cognitive liberty, manipulation and emulated empathy, sycophancy as harm, people's own agency. Boundary: Meaning goes to Meaning; AI-safety workers to People; information integrity to Sensemaking. Examples: ai-psychological-research-coalition, the-human-line-project, common-sense-media.
- `meaning` **Meaning, dignity & formation**: [Human minds & meaning] Humanity's moral and spiritual orientation to AI: what it means to be human, dignity and flourishing, moral and character formation, and the ethical voice of religious, humanist and contemplative communities to their members and publics. Boundary: Insight turned into model specs is What AI should value; red lines go to Rights or Security. Examples: ai-and-faith, holy-see, delta.
- `people` **People doing the work**: [Human minds & meaning] The people who build, govern, fund and make AI safe: wellbeing, inner development, practical wisdom, conscience, oaths, whistleblowing, worker voice in labs, and the data-labelling and rating workforce; also widening who does this work. Boundary: Public mental health is Protecting minds; labour as counter-power is Power. Examples: rethink-wellbeing, the-ai-whistleblower-initiative, monastic-academy-for-the-preservation-of-life-on.
- `epistemics` **Sensemaking, truth & foresight**: [Living & deciding together] Whether people and institutions can see clearly with AI: forecasting, foresight and early warning, reasoning tools, model truthfulness, fact-checking, provenance, epistemic commons, information integrity, and how publics picture and narrate AI futures. Boundary: Deciding together is Democratic voice; individual cognitive harm is Protecting minds. Examples: forecasting-research-institute, future-of-life-foundation, sophron-research.
- `democracy` **Democratic voice & legitimacy**: [Living & deciding together] How societies decide together about and with AI: assemblies and sortition, deliberative polling, public input into model behaviour, AI-assisted deliberation and bridging, collective intelligence, civil liberties, polycentric governance. Boundary: Facts and forecasts are Sensemaking; legal accountability is Rights. Examples: collective-intelligence-project, computational-democracy-project, stanford-deliberative-democracy-lab.
- `justice` **Rights & plural voices**: [Living & deciding together] Whose interests and voices count, and how developers are held to account: human-rights and liability law, bias and accountability audits, data and cultural sovereignty, Indigenous rights, Global South voice, feminist and decolonial alternatives. Boundary: Participation procedures are Democratic voice; competition and labour are Power. Examples: center-for-ai-and-digital-policy, un-human-rights-b-tech-project, global-center-on-ai-governance.
- `power` **Power, wealth & work**: [Living & deciding together] Who holds power and wealth as AI takes over labour and judgement: concentration and lock-in, gradual disempowerment, windfall distribution and TAI economics, competition policy, public AI, data ownership, labour as counter-power. Boundary: Litigation for specific harms is Rights; data-worker wellbeing is People. Examples: alignment-of-complex-systems-research-group, formation-research, windfall-trust.
- `multi_agent` **Multi-agent AI & cooperation**: [Cooperation, conflict & civilisation] Populations of interacting AI agents and the cooperation science that governs them: cooperative AI, collusion, bargaining and commitment, emergent collective behaviour, multi-agent security, game-theoretic models of AI races. Boundary: Rivalry of states or labs studied diplomatically is Security. Examples: cooperative-ai-foundation, foundations-of-cooperative-ai-lab, center-on-long-term-risk.
- `security` **Security & international coordination**: [Cooperation, conflict & civilisation] Keeping states, militaries and developers from catastrophic conflict and misuse: military AI and nuclear escalation, autonomous weapons, bio and cyber misuse, influence operations, US-China and Track II dialogue, UN processes, treaties, red lines. Boundary: AI-to-AI conflict is Multi-agent AI. Examples: safe-ai-forum, stockholm-international-peace-research-institute, securebio.

# Tagging guide
TAGGING GUIDE v1. Read the facet definitions first; this says how to apply them, one roster entry at a time.

A. GROUND RULES
A1 Unit = the roster entry. For a unit inside a host (Anthropic Model Welfare, UK AISI's societal-resilience team) tag that unit, not the host's portfolio.
A2 Evidence = its own AI outputs in 2025-26 (roster 'what it is' and 'AI connection', then its site). Never reputation or what it 'should' do, except the one-line method required for ring = potential.
A3 Bring vs aim: tradition, sub_tradition and way_of_knowing describe what it BRINGS; problem, quadrant, level, framing and transfer describe what its AI work is ABOUT and whom it changes. Geodesic brings narrative (Arts), knows empirically and works on What AI should value.
A4 Tag these facets: tradition (home first, then 0-2 secondary), sub_tradition, ring, problems (1-3, primary first), quadrants (1-2), roles (1-3), org_type, transfer, framing, way_of_knowing, region, standpoint, inta_hubs, engagement, status; badges optional. Not tagged (derived later): family, problem arc, span, classic-map status.
A5 In tag_notes: one sentence of evidence each for home, ring and transfer; cite the rule code when a tie-breaker decided; for ring = potential, name the transferable method in one line.
A6 Never invent a category: take the closest and write 'MISFIT: <why>' in tag_notes (feeds the gap register).

B. HOME TRADITION (first value of tradition)
List its AI outputs and which discipline's methods, concepts, people or practices produced them, then apply in order:
H1 Method over topic: a psychology lab studying religious users is Psychology; a law clinic suing over chatbot harms is Law.
H2 Imported concepts: ML, AI governance and policy analysis are not sectors; if they are its own method, place it by the outside tradition whose concepts it imports (Geodesic Arts, TruthfulAI Philosophy, AE Studio Life & systems, KIRA Economics).
H3 Constituency: publications, professional bodies, unions and federations go with the community they serve (Bulletin Peace, Tricycle Religion, Integral Life Contemplative, APA Psychology, unions Economics, Digital Minds Newsletter AI minds); general-audience journalism, film and campaigns go to Arts.
H4 Funders: one-programme funders go to that programme's tradition (Carnegie Corporation Peace, Amaranth Life & systems, Khyentse Religion). Generalist funders (rule K) get a nominal home from the most distinctive line in their entry and are drawn in the funder band.
H5 Hubs: the tradition its AI output draws on most; if tied, its leadership's discipline.
Then the boundary rules, which override first instinct:
B1 Religion vs Contemplative (Buddhist test): speaking from, for or to a tradition's texts, institutions or community (federations, translation, faith media and funders, rulings, convening a faith community) is Religion: Buddhism & AI Initiative, 84000, Mindstream, WFB. A research or design artefact built from contemplative insight, or practice training for AI people regardless of faith, is Contemplative: CSAS, Arising, Mathematical Metaphysics Institute, FLIP, MAPLE, Wisdom + AI. Integral, metamodern and Teilhardian bodies are Contemplative.
B2 Psychology vs Contemplative: third-person wisdom, flourishing or wellbeing research is Psychology (Wisdom and Culture Lab, Greater Good); meditation-based training and contemplative science are Contemplative (CCARE, Garrison). Services for AI-safety workers (CFAR, ESPR, Rethink Wellbeing, Upgradable) are Psychology unless they are contemplative practice (MAPLE).
B3 Psychology vs AI minds: people attributing minds to AI, clinical lens Psychology (Human Line Project), led by consciousness or welfare researchers AI minds (WhenAISeemsConscious, Cambridge Digital Minds).
B4 Life & systems vs AI minds: neuroscience used to build or read AI is Life & systems; asking whether AI or animals have experience is AI minds, whatever the method (Sussex).
B5 Life & systems vs Psychology: brains and organisms Life & systems; cognitive science and experiments on models Psychology (Re-Align, IRIS, Helmholtz Munich).
B6 Formal vs Life & systems: proofs and mathematical theory of learning and agents Formal (Iliad, Simplex, CAIF, CLR); complexity, emergence, agent-based simulation, cybernetics Life & systems (XOR Labs, ACS, Concordia).
B7 Metacrisis: systems diagnosis Life & systems (Consilience, Club of Rome, Game B); inner-development or metamodern method Contemplative (Perspectiva, Life Itself, IDG).
B8 Indigenous & decolonial vs Law, Economics, Democracy: knowledge tradition, not geography or identity. Indigenous-led knowledge and data sovereignty, or decolonial, Black or feminist epistemology as the core contribution, stay (Te Hiku, GIDA, DAIR); rights law, policy, advocacy, political economy or civic tech by Global South or feminist groups go to those sectors (GCG, IT for Change, SUPERRR, Tattle), with location in region and standpoint.
B9 Law vs Economics vs Psychology: power, competition, labour and commons Economics; rights, liability, audits, STS, ELSI Law; children's-rights law Law, child-development science Psychology; AI-rights advocacy argued as law Law.
B10 Democracy vs Arts: probabilistic forecasting, argument mapping, fact-checking Democracy; scenarios, foresight, wargames, technology assessment Arts.
B11 Democracy vs Peace: deliberative tools used by peacebuilders in conflict settings, and religious-founded peace research, are Peace (Toda, UN DPPA, CMI).
B12 Safety vs Peace: biosecurity evaluation, health security, catastrophe resilience Safety; nonproliferation and arms control Peace.
B13 Philosophy vs AI minds: philosophy of mind mainly about AI consciousness AI minds (Essentia); moral philosophy with AI welfare as one strand Philosophy (CRS). Ethics councils Philosophy.

C. SECONDARY TRADITIONS: after the home, 0-2 values (3 for hubs; up to 4 for generalist funders, listing what they fund most), only for methods, staff or formal partners, never mere topic, never ML or AI policy. For a seam entry the first secondary value is the seam it is drawn on.
D. SUB-TRADITION: only among the home's sub-traditions (key prefix = home); if two fit, the one matching its main AI output.
E. RING: keep the roster value unless evidence contradicts it; say why if you change it. (1) Founding or primary mission is making advanced AI go well, inside the AI-safety/x-risk ecosystem: core. (2) Otherwise no sustained AI programme in 2024-26 (standing programme, funded project, fellowship, publication line, repeated convening): potential. (3) Programme explicitly framed around safety, catastrophic risk, alignment, superintelligence, AI welfare or 'AI going well': bridge; else adjacent. Old institutions with new AI-safety programmes are bridge however large; new AI-safety organisations founded by people from other fields are core.
F. PROBLEM: primary first (test: if it vanished, which column loses most?); others only for sustained lines of work. Must-pair: values + democracy when public input yields a model spec (CIP, MAI); minds + ai_status when work spans attribution and welfare; assurance + the substantive problem when it builds evaluations (CEFEAI, Common Sense). Funders: the problems of the work they fund.
G. QUADRANT: the object of the work, never the home. Single AI systems, brains, artefacts It; many agents, institutions, markets, geopolitics, ecologies Its; experience, wellbeing, character, possible AI interiority I; values, meaning, culture, narrative, religion, dialogue We. 1-2, main first; 3-4 only for explicitly integral frameworks. FLIP It+I; CEFEAI It+We; Formation Its; CHT Its+I.
H. ROLE: 1-3 by share of effort. Fund only if it gives money to others; Educate needs teaching or formation (events alone are Convene).
I. ORG TYPE: units inside hosts Programme; university units and public research institutes Academic; unions, societies, denominations, federations Membership body; agencies, statutory councils, UN/OECD/OIC bodies, AI-safety institutes Public body.
J. TRANSFER: 'who or what changes because of this work?' First that fits its PRIMARY purpose: (1) AI systems: Into the model; (2) the people who build, govern or fund AI, as people: Into the builders; (3) governance instruments: Into institutions; (4) its own constituency protected from AI: Protecting; (5) its own community informed and convened: Convening; (6) ring potential with no AI work at all: None yet, never otherwise. Common Sense Media's ratings exist to protect children, so Protecting. Funders: the work they fund.
