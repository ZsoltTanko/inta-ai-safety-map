## peace
LANDSCAPE. In peace, conflict and security, AI-relevant knowledge sits in four communities. Their distance from AI safety varies a lot.

(1) Nuclear risk and arms control: SIPRI, ELN, Pugwash, NTI, Bulletin, IST, CISAC, UChicago XLab. These have the deepest analogous expertise: confidence-building measures, hotlines, fail-safe reviews, verification, NC3. They engage AI almost entirely through 'AI in nuclear command and control', not through frontier-model loss of control. IST's Recursive Self-Improvement Initiative is the exception. Carnegie Corporation of New York funds most of this bridge (SIPRI, the Pugwash-Carnegie Fellow, IST's AI-Nuclear Policy Accelerator). The July 2026 Vatican-hosted Nobel Laureates Assembly and its Rome Declaration showed the community converging on AI, with lab staff present.

(2) Track II and private diplomacy: SAIF/IDAIS, Brookings–Tsinghua CISS, HD Centre, Oxford Martin AIGI, OCPL, Concordia, AISA. This is the most mature bridge. It quietly prepared the ground for the May 2026 Trump–Xi agreement on an intergovernmental AI dialogue and for the US proposal (Sept 2026) of an AI-incident alert channel. NPR (Sept 2026) also names CSIS, the National Committee on US-China Relations, Samm Sacks and Jack Shanahan in Track Two AI talks. I could not confirm a dedicated NCUSCR AI programme, so it is not listed.

(3) Multilateral and humanitarian law: UNIDIR RAISE/AISE, the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance (first session July 2026), GC REAIM, the CCW autonomous-weapons process (128 states agreed a definition in Sept 2026; treaty talks sought at the Nov 2026 CCW Review Conference), ICRC, Stop Killer Robots/ICRAC. 'Meaningful human control' is a well-developed legal doctrine of human oversight over irreversible automated action, and it maps directly onto AI-control debates. These communities deliberately distance themselves from x-risk framing, though, and the Bulletin also runs anti-'doomer' pieces. The two lineages, AI ethics and AI safety, are in quiet tension here. REAIM is losing momentum: 40 endorsers in 2026 against 60+ in 2024, with the US, China and India abstaining.

(4) Peacebuilding and mediation: Build Up, AI for Peace, the UN DPPA Innovation Cell, ACCORD, KAIPTC, the Alliance for Middle East Peace collective dialogues (Konya, Schirch, Procaccia, Bakker; FAccT 2025). Almost all of this is 'AI for peace' (applications: social listening, AI-mediated consultations), not 'peace for AI'. Their core knowledge is conflict transformation, dialogue facilitation across enemies, trauma-informed practice and insider mediation, and it is essentially absent from AI safety. Search for Common Ground and Beyond Conflict, both on the starting list, showed no AI engagement that I could find. They are absences, not candidates.

GAPS FOR FOUNDERS AND FUNDERS:
(a) Nobody applies conflict-transformation methods to the AI race itself: between frontier labs (a lab-to-lab Track 1.5), between the AI-ethics and AI-safety camps, or between accelerationists and safety advocates. This is a direct int/a fit (inner work, dialogue, 'don't exacerbate the race').
(b) Arms-control verification expertise (IAEA, CTBTO, OPCW, VERTIC) has barely been moved into compute governance and AI-treaty verification. A placement fellowship modelled on IST's accelerator, but for verification experts, would be cheap and high-leverage.
(c) Negotiation science (the GCSP PIN programme, Harvard PON) is not connected to cooperative-AI and multi-agent safety work (CAIF, CLR are on the map), though AI agents will bargain and international AI deals need design.
(d) The wargaming and crisis-simulation tradition (Hoover's Wargaming and Crisis Simulation Initiative, CSIS Futures Lab's foreign-policy LLM benchmark, King's College wargaming) studies LLM escalation bias. I could not verify their current status: the fetches were rate-limited. Worth a follow-up as a 'safety evals from security studies' bridge.
(e) Peace-research data producers (PRIO, Uppsala UCDP, ACLED) do not systematically monitor AI's effects on conflict and escalation. An 'AI conflict observatory' is missing.
(f) Africa and Latin America are near-absent. ACCORD and KAIPTC touch only AI-for-peacebuilding, and AISA (Manila) is the only Global South AI-safety convenor found.
(g) Faith-based peace actors (Vatican, Soka Gakkai, G20 Interfaith Forum, World Council of Churches) are converging on AI plus nuclear. A natural meeting point with int/a's wisdom and inner-development framing.
(h) Funding is concentrated: Carnegie Corporation for AI–nuclear, Coefficient Giving for Simon Institute and similar, and a search result lists Packard as a SAIF funder (unverified). MacArthur's exit from nuclear left a gap. New funders could back peacebuilding–AI bridges and early-career US-China safety exchanges below the elite IDAIS level.

SURPRISES: The Elders commissioned a scoping of a full AI-governance programme (Dec 2025 to May 2026) and co-sponsored the September 2026 treaty forum. Pugwash, the model for IDAIS, is now itself running AI workshops. HD's 2021 draft Code of Conduct on military AI predates most lab safety frameworks.

## neuro
LANDSCAPE. This angle has three clusters that barely talk to each other.

(1) NeuroAI for safety: brain-inspired alignment, brain data, emulation.
- Intellectually rich but institutionally thin. The reference document is Amaranth's 2024-25 'NeuroAI for AI Safety' roadmap (Mineault). The only standing grant line is Foresight's neurotech/WBE track.
- The researchers who actually do this work sit in a few places: Astera (Byrnes's social-instinct agenda, Simplex's belief-geometry interpretability), AE Studio (self-other overlap, attention schema), Re-Align (representational alignment), and two non-Western poles: Yi Zeng's BrainCog in Beijing and Japan's WBAI/ALIGN, whose framing is 'symbiosis' rather than control.
- Big caveat: almost every brain-inspired effort is also an AGI or capabilities programme. Examples are Astera Obelisk ($1B+), WBAI (Toyota, Toshiba, Panasonic), BrainCog's 'living AGI' and Eon's WBE race. Mineault's 'differential path' framing is the right lens, and funders need int/a-style discernment here.

(2) Consciousness science, AI welfare and digital minds.
- The fastest-growing and best-networked cluster. 2025 saw a burst: PRISM, CIMC, Eleos's first ConCon, Rethink Priorities' Digital Consciousness Model, Cambridge Digital Minds (3,600+ applicants for 15 fellowship places) and the Longview/Navigation/Macroscopic Digital Sentience Consortium.
- It is EA-funded and mostly philosophy-led. Mainstream consciousness science engages only partly: Sussex/Seth, AMCS's UN submission, FAU. The big societies (ASSC; its 2027 meeting is in Beijing) and the big neuroscience funders (Templeton, Kavli, Simons, Allen) have no AI programme.
- Conscium is a telling case: it launched as a 'machine consciousness' company and pivoted to enterprise agent verification.

(3) Cognitive science of values, agency and human-AI behaviour.
- Academic and scattered: Bramley/Summerfield's 'Cognitive science of AI alignment' (CogSci 2026), MPIB's Center for Humans and Machines, and Re-Align. No dedicated organisation exists.

WHERE KNOWLEDGE LIVES THAT SAFETY ISN'T USING
- Affective and social neuroscience (hypothalamic and brainstem circuits for empathy and social reward). This is exactly what Byrnes's agenda needs, and it is essentially one person's project.
- Metacognition and introspection research. It bears directly on deception, situational awareness and honest self-report evals, and so far reaches safety only via welfare work (Eleos, Rethink Priorities).
- Developmental cognitive science of how children acquire norms.
- Predictive processing and active inference. These have alignment-relevant theory but a disconnected, partly commercial community (Active Inference Institute, VERSES).
- Contemplative neuroscience (Monash M3CS, the Laukkonen/Chandaria 'Contemplative AI' programme): a handful of papers, no programme.
- Animal sentience science (LSE Coller Centre, NYU CMEP). This is the methodological template for AI welfare.

GAPS A FOUNDER OR FUNDER COULD FILL
- No institutional home for NeuroAI safety. There is no lab, fellowship or RFP built on the roadmap, and Amaranth's follow-through is not visible. A 'NeuroAI Safety Fellowship' could run through Neuromatch, which already reaches 30k researchers in 137 countries.
- No funding to reverse-engineer human social instincts as an alignment target. Social-neuroscience labs could be funded to work with Byrnes.
- No bridge from consciousness science to safety (as opposed to welfare). Self-models, metacognition and global-workspace-like architectures bear on deception and situational awareness, but no one runs 'introspection and metacognition evals' with evals orgs.
- The safety-welfare tension is acknowledged (NYU CMEP's Welfare Alignment Project, Eleos) but almost no one designs control measures that are also humane. This is a natural care-ethics project for int/a.
- Protecting human minds is thin. There is no neuroscience or psychiatry group on AI-induced delusion, attachment, or false attribution of consciousness, beyond the small WhenAISeemsConscious guide and Seth's warnings. Neurorights covers neural data, not chatbots.
- Big science funders and societies (Templeton, Kavli, Simons, Allen, ASSC) have no AI-safety window. ASSC 2027 in Beijing and MoC7 in Copenhagen (Oct 2026) are openings for East-West dialogue on consciousness and AI.
- No independent referee for WBE and brain-inspired-AGI claims, for example scrutinising Eon's 91% figure or whether neuro-inspired designs are differentially safe. Carboncopies does informal due diligence.

SURPRISES
- QRI, despite its EA roots, has no visible AI programme in 2026.
- AE Studio's alignment podcast started in 2025, not 2026 as one secondary source claimed.
- Japan's alignment community is led by brain-inspired-AGI researchers.

- WebFetch on astera.org and ae.studio was rate-limited. Astera claims come from search snippets of its own pages plus Byrnes's Alignment Forum profile. AE Studio claims come from its LessWrong profile, search snippets of its own pages, and the Digital Minds Newsletter's 2025 review.

Anthropic's model-welfare team is excluded as a lab.

## minds
LANDSCAPE. Knowledge in this angle sits in seven communities that barely talk to the AI-safety field:
(1) developmental neuroscience and adolescent psychology: UNC Winston Center, APA, Children and Screens, Digital Wellness Lab;
(2) psychiatry and clinical work: Stanford Brainstorm, AIPRC, King's College London's research on AI-associated delusions, and individual clinicians such as Keith Sakata at UCSF;
(3) child-rights and consumer advocacy: Fairplay, Common Sense, 5Rights/LSE DFC, UNICEF Innocenti, Human Change;
(4) experimental HCI and behavioural science: MIT AHA, Max Planck CHM;
(5) philosophy of autonomy: Oxford HAI Lab, Cosmos;
(6) law: Tech Justice Law Project;
(7) lived-experience mutual aid: the Human Line Project.

The most striking finding: of 28 entries, only UK AISI is on the aisafety.com map. CHT, Common Sense Media and the companion-harm groups are absent, although they arguably shape how frontier models behave with minors (teen modes, crisis protocols) more directly than many entries that are on the map.

BRIDGES THAT EXIST:
- AISI's 'socioaffective alignment' paper (Kirk, Gabriel, Summerfield et al.) is the main theoretical bridge: it treats attachment and companion dynamics as an alignment problem.
- MIT AHA worked with OpenAI on the study of affective use, and works on cognitive debt and sycophancy.
- Stanford Brainstorm works with Stanford's Center for AI Safety and with MLCommons.
- Common Sense's risk ratings of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Meta AI work as de facto external evals.
- everyone.AI's iRAISE coalition puts developmental scientists in a room with OpenAI, Anthropic and Google.
- AIPRC's founding partnership with CHT links clinical documentation with incentive and race analysis.
- Litigation (TJLP) turns harms into design duties.
- Zak Stein (AIPRC) is the clearest int/a-native figure here: a metamodern developmental educator who frames AI psychosis as 'attachment hacking at scale'.

MISSING BRIDGES (gaps for founders and funders):
(a) Clinical cases into evals. Human Line has about 185 documented cases and AIPRC collects user submissions, but nobody turns consented, de-identified case corpora into multi-turn evals for sycophancy and delusion reinforcement that AISIs and labs can run. This is a concrete, fundable translation organisation.
(b) No longitudinal developmental cohort on AI exposure. Current evidence is cross-sectional, drawn from Reddit (Drexel), or from one-year adult panels. Funders could add an AI module to existing cohorts such as ABCD, Millennium Cohort or Growing Up in Ireland.
(c) No clinician curriculum or validated screening instrument for AI-related conditions. Canadian psychiatrists are being urged to screen for 'high-risk human-AI engagement' with no tools, and the PIMU clinicians (Digital Wellness Lab) have not been brought in.
(d) Developmental psychology as alignment theory. Attachment, caregiving and stage theories of moral and epistemic development (Kegan, Fischer, Gilligan's care ethics) almost never inform model character or the question of what a 'good companion' is. A 'developmental alignment' research programme would be int/a-native and would fill the seed list's 'care-based alignment' gap.
(e) No observatory of adult cognitive sovereignty. MIT's cognitive-debt EEG work, Sapien Labs' population data and the Oxford HAI Lab's design work are not connected to the 'gradual disempowerment' framing (ACS, on the classic map). Population metrics of how well people keep their capabilities are missing.
(f) Funding is siloed. Funders here are small (Young Futures about $1.4M, Children and Screens about $150k, AHA micro-grants of $5–10k), and I found no sign that x-risk funders (Coefficient Giving, SFF, LTFF) fund this space; treat that as an impression, not verified. Meanwhile the child-safety coalition has political power x-risk advocacy lacks: a 26-state intergovernmental coalition, state laws, lawsuits. There is room for coalition-building, but the frames differ. Child safety tends toward age-gating and content filters; x-risk tends toward capability and incentive constraints.
(g) Geographic gaps. Companion-AI use is massive in China, Japan and Korea, but I found no independent psychological-harm research or advocacy groups there; my search was English-only, so this may be a blind spot rather than a true gap. The Global South appears mainly through LSE's RIGHTS.AI and UNICEF.
(h) Older adults, a major market for companion robots and chatbots, have almost no psychological-harm watchdog. The Oxford Institute for Ethics in AI (Caroline Green, on the seed list) is the exception.
(i) Attention and contemplative communities (Friends of Attention) have not engaged with AI. CHT is the only major bridge from attention critique to AI.

SURPRISES AND TENSIONS:
- Fairplay's call for a five-year pause on generative AI in schools is the most 'speed of wisdom' policy position found anywhere in this angle.
- Cosmos and the HAI Lab defend human autonomy from a classical-liberal, pro-builder stance, so they share concerns with int/a while differing in worldview.
- AIPRC was announced as 'AI Psychological Harms Research Coalition' (aiphrc.org) but now presents as the 'AI Psychological Research Coalition' (aiprc.org), which suggests a deliberate softening of its framing.

METHOD CAVEATS:
- The web-search cap shared across this session ran out after about 23 queries, so later candidates were checked only by fetching their own sites.
- Common Sense Media's site returned 403, so that entry rests on its press releases and press coverage.
- The Paris Peace Forum page was rate-limited.
- Candidates I could not verify well enough to include, and so left out: Hopelab, Joan Ganz Cooney Center, Instituto Alana (Brazil), Molly Rose Foundation, Ada Lovelace Institute, Psychology of Technology Institute, USC Neely Center, Kinsey Institute, Institute for Family Studies, AAP Center of Excellence, Spring Health's VERA-MH benchmark (a company product), Character.AI's announced independent AI safety lab, and the Humanity AI philanthropic coalition. Each is worth a follow-up check.

## philosophy
LANDSCAPE. This angle splits into four clusters that barely talk to one another.

(1) AI moral status and digital minds: Eleos, NYU CMEP, PRISM, Sentience Institute, Sentient Futures, and Longview's 2026 Digital Minds RFP (up to $2M grants). This is the best-connected cluster: EA-funded, close to the labs, and growing fast in 2025–26. Almost none of it is on the classic aisafety.com map.

(2) Academic philosophy of AI: Oxford IEAI, HAI Lab, Cosmos, MINT (now at Johns Hopkins), Leverhulme CFI, Uehiro, FAU, Copenhagen CPAI, ESDiT in the Netherlands, DIGHUM in Vienna, and Berggruen with Noema. Most of it is framed as 'AI ethics' or 'human flourishing', not x-risk. The real bridges are MINT (normative-competence evals, AGI Governance Fellowship, a DeepMind visiting role), Cosmos/HAI Lab (autonomy and truth-seeking tools, Anthropic links), CFI (a futures-and-responsibility programme) and Berggruen (Noema's 'AI Safety Is A Global Public Good', plus a China hub).

(3) Faith and theology. This is the 2025–26 surprise: an inflection point.
- The Vatican issued Antiqua et Nova (January 2025) and Leo XIV's first encyclical, Magnifica Humanitas (May 2026). The encyclical calls for an international framework to curb the AI arms race, rejects handing lethal decisions to AI, and warns about power concentrating in a few labs. Chris Olah attended the launch.
- FLI has quietly become the largest funder of faith-based AI-risk work: a $1.5M call with 16 grants totalling $1.36M (WCC, Sinai and Synapses, Gospel Coalition, Lausanne, Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of America, MENA Islamic scholars, IBA Karachi, Hindu perspectives through Impact Academy/SteadRise, ILINA's African Faith & AI Initiative, Project Imago Dei, Wheaton College). FLI also funds AI and Faith with IST.
- Catholic universities are building infrastructure: Notre Dame's DELTA summit and grants, and Santa Clara's ITEC with a Vatican dicastery.

(4) Funders. The John Templeton Foundation's $60M+ Intelligence Venture (from 2026) and TWCF's Diverse Intelligences hubs are large pools with no safety framing.

MISSING BRIDGES (gaps for founders and funders):
(a) Faith engagement stops short of AGI. Magnifica Humanitas never mentions superintelligence or loss of control, and most church work is on dignity, labour, war, children and companionship. No one acts as a standing translator between theological anthropology and loss-of-control and power-concentration arguments. There is also no permanent interreligious AI-risk council comparable to the faith coalitions in nuclear disarmament. FLI's grants are one-year seeds.
(b) No organisation mines moral and religious traditions for model specifications. Labs now write constitutions and train character, often implicitly on virtue ethics, but no philosophy or faith body systematically reviews model specs or builds evals for wisdom and virtue across traditions. CTNS's 'Virtuous AI' project ended in 2025, and DELTA works on forming people, not models. This is the clearest founder opportunity in this angle (it overlaps the seed list's 'wisdom evals' gap).
(c) Theology and digital minds are on a collision course with no venue between them: Catholic teaching denies AI moral agency while the AI-welfare field takes AI moral patienthood seriously. CMEP's paper on the safety–welfare tension shows integrative work is needed even inside the welfare cluster.
(d) Non-Western philosophy is thin. Islamic AI ethics is mostly events (CILE) plus FLI micro-grants. Buddhist work amounts to the Buddhism & AI Initiative and the seed list's CSAS. Confucian and Chinese philosophy appears only through Berggruen China and Beijing-AISI (on the classic map). Hindu and African work exists only as single FLI grants.
(e) No academic home for 'what should the long-term future be' philosophy since Oxford's Future of Humanity Institute (closed 2024) and Global Priorities Institute (closed 2025). Forethought partly fills the gap, but outside a university.
(f) Templeton's money is the largest untapped lever. An int/a-flavoured consortium on wisdom, moral status and collective intelligence could plausibly win Intelligence Venture funding in later cycles.

Methodological caveat: the session-wide web-search budget ran out after 14 queries, so most candidates were verified by fetching their own sites. I could not verify these and left them out, though they are worth checking: Lingnan's Hong Kong Catastrophic Risk Centre (philosophy-based x-risk), HKU's AI & Humanity Lab (Herman Cappelen), Duke's Moral AI project, the California Institute for Machine Consciousness (Joscha Bach; site did not render), Rethink Priorities' digital-consciousness and moral-weight work, the LSE Jeremy Coller Centre for Animal Sentience (Jonathan Birch, AI sentience), Cambridge Muslim College's AI work, and the Builders AI Forum at the Gregorian University. I left out the Builders AI Forum on scope: it builds Catholic AI products for evangelisation, not work on making AI go well.

## sociotech
LANDSCAPE. Within law, rights and sociotechnical governance, the knowledge that AI safety under-uses clusters in eight places.
(1) System-safety engineering and accident analysis: Dobbe at TU Delft, AI Now's safety-critical engineering stance, MIT's STAMP/STPA group, and the AI Incident Database's aviation-style incident learning.
(2) Normative and political philosophy and legal theory: MINT Lab, Hadfield's Normativity Lab, the Knight Institute, Oxford's Institute for Ethics in AI, Schwartz Reisman and Edinburgh's Technomoral Futures. Their concepts include legitimacy, democratic authority, normative competence and the legal infrastructure agents will need.
(3) International human-rights law and business and human rights: OHCHR B-Tech's due-diligence frame for foundation models, CAIDP, the Council of Europe AI Convention, and the new IBA AI Institute.
(4) Tort and product-liability litigation: Tech Justice Law Project's Character.AI and OpenAI cases are currently the fastest-moving accountability mechanism for harms to human minds.
(5) Participatory and community auditing and red-teaming: Humane Intelligence, AIAL, Data & Society's AIMLab, AlgorithmWatch, and the new UW Sociotechnical Safety Lab.
(6) Global Majority perspectives: GCG, Digital Futures Lab, Research ICT Africa, Derechos Digitales and DAIR.
(7) Humanitarian disarmament: Stop Killer Robots, with UNGA majorities of 156 states and the UN Secretary-General and ICRC 'moral red line' call.
(8) Political economy and labour: AI Now and Data & Society. Their power-concentration analysis is what x-risk work on gradual disempowerment and lock-in keeps rediscovering without citing it.

EXISTING BRIDGES.
- MINT Lab's 'sociotechnical AI safety' grant and workshops at Stanford and Kioloa. Seth Lazar now sits at Johns Hopkins beside Gillian Hadfield, so JHU is becoming a hub for normative alignment.
- The Knight Institute's AI and Democratic Freedoms essays.
- IASEAI (already on the classic map), the one venue built to host both camps. Oxford's Institute for Ethics in AI became an affiliate in 2026.
- GCG's African Hub on AI Safety, Peace and Security. Rachel Adams now leads both GCG and Cambridge's Leverhulme CFI.
- Digital Futures Lab's 'AI safety in the Global South' stream.
- Humane Intelligence and UW in Microsoft's EXTRA red-team alliance.
- HPI's new DFG-funded AI Safety and Societal Impacts group in Potsdam/Berlin.
- PAI's Safety-Critical AI programme.
- AI Now's July 2026 'Double Agents' paper on agentic cyber risk. The ethics camp is moving onto safety ground.

EVIDENCE THE DIVIDE IS STRUCTURAL.
- Roytburg and Miller ('Mind the Gap', IASEAI 2026): over 80% of collaborations stay within either the safety or the ethics community, and about 5% of papers carry over 85% of bridging links. Bridging rests on a handful of brokers and is fragile.
- Gyevnár and Kasirzadeh (arXiv, March 2026) propose 'critical bridging' around shared problems: transparency, reproducibility and weak governance mechanisms. This maps closely onto int/a's 'decoupling and recoupling' principle.
- The classic aisafety.com map has no entry mentioning human rights, children or the Global South (apart from ILINA), and only MAI mentions democracy. Its law entries (LawAI, CLAIR, LASST, Legal Safety Lab) all come from inside the x-risk world.

MISSING BRIDGES (gaps for founders and funders).
(a) A 'bridging problems' fellowship or clinic. It would pair accountability and ethics researchers with safety researchers on shared, concrete problems: AI companions and psychological harm, accountability for agents, evaluation validity, and regulatory capture. Without it, bridging depends on a few overstretched brokers.
(b) A litigation-to-safety translation desk. It would systematically mine discovery documents, expert testimony and remedies from chatbot-harm cases (TJLP, SMVLC) as evidence on frontier labs' safety practice. No safety org does this.
(c) Human-rights due diligence as a vehicle for frontier-risk management. The UNGPs framework plus High Commissioner Türk's September 2026 letter to frontier AI companies is an opening that no org yet owns.
(d) System-safety engineering (STAMP/STPA) applied to agentic deployments and lab safety culture. The discipline is mature but almost absent from AI safety; Dobbe's critique of the International AI Safety Report is an isolated voice.
(e) A shared venue where humanitarian-disarmament campaigners and AI red-lines advocates can learn from each other, since Stop Killer Robots has built exactly the coalition machinery AI safety lacks.
(f) Global Majority AI-safety capacity beyond South Africa and India. Latin America has rights organisations but no AI-safety hub.
(g) Children's-rights design codes (5Rights) feeding into model specs and evals.
(h) Funding apartheid. Humanity AI ($500M over five years), the European AI & Society Fund and Mozilla fund one side; Coefficient Giving and SFF fund the other; there is virtually no co-funding. A jointly funded bridging pool is the obvious play, and the newly arriving funders in the brief are well placed to broker it.

SURPRISES AND CAUTIONS.
- The UN rights chief explicitly addressed frontier models in September 2026.
- The IBA's new AI Institute names 'frontier AI systems' as a priority.
- BRAID is £18M+ and runs to 2028, a ready template for a humanities-to-safety programme.
- Some organisations here (DAIR, and parts of AI Now) openly reject the x-risk and EA paradigm ('TESCREAL'). Bridging requires epistemic humility and non-instrumental engagement, which suits int/a's 'speed of wisdom' and 'full-spectrum knowing' principles.

VERIFICATION LIMITS. The shared web-search budget ran out after 18 queries. Some primary sites returned 403 or were rate-limited: LCFI, Mozilla, CDT, OHCHR, and parts of Edinburgh. Those entries carry medium confidence. CDT's AI Governance Lab, Princeton CITP, Weizenbaum Institute, HIIG and the Minderoo Centre were considered but not verified, so they are left out.

## economics
LANDSCAPE. This angle has four clusters that seldom cite each other.
(1) Cooperative AI and multi-agent game theory: CAIF, FOCAL, CLR, Modeling Cooperation, Hadfield's Normativity Lab, Gradient Institute, plus the behavioural evidence from MPIB's Center for Humans and Machines. It is the best-funded cluster in 2026. CAIF's $10m fund with Schmidt Sciences, DeepMind and ARIA (awards due autumn 2026) and a new CEO make it the obvious home for agent-ecosystem safety.
(2) Economics of transformative AI and post-AGI economics: EconTAI (UVA), Stanford Digital Economy Lab, the Stripe fellowship, Windfall Trust, Convergence's AGI Social Contract work, and since 2026 lab- and state-side bodies: the DeepMind Institute (Sep 2026; 'Economic policy for AGI') and the UK AI Economics Institute (June 2026, chaired by Simon Johnson). Anthropic's Economic Futures fund publishes usage data.
(3) Power concentration and political economy, in two traditions that barely speak. The x-risk side: Forethought (AI-enabled coups), Formation (lock-in, secret loyalties; $2.15m from Coefficient Giving), Longview's Extreme Power Concentration RFP (May 2026), MINT Lab. The critical and democratic side: AI Now, Open Markets/OMI Europe, Public AI Network/Metagov, RadicalxChange, Platform Cooperativism, and in the Global South Aapti and Research ICT Africa, funded in Europe by EAISF. AI Now openly dismisses 'doomerism', yet its diagnosis of concentrated power is close to Longview's.
(4) Gradual disempowerment is the synthesising frame. ACS (Prague) and the post-agi.org workshops, with Duvenaud at Toronto, are the only places that deliberately bring economists, political scientists and alignment researchers into one room.

SURPRISES. 'Cooperative AI' has two meanings: CAIF's game-theoretic agent cooperation and the cooperative movement's democratic ownership of AI. PCC held a 'Cooperative AI' conference in 2025 and 'Solidarity AI' in 2026, with no contact with CAIF. Int/a's observation that 'cooperative AI' people are int/a-flavoured applies to both, and pairing them is an easy convening win. 'Lock-in' likewise means market lock-in at OMI Europe and civilisational lock-in at Formation. Equilibria Network, on the classic map, has a parked domain.

LOSSES. Stanford's TAI economics course is paused in 2026. Oxford's Global Priorities Institute, long the academic home of long-termist economics, closed in 2025 (from memory; not re-checked in this session). FLI's $4m power-concentration grant round (2023-24; OpenMined, Nesta's Centre for Collective Intelligence Design, Mila and others) is finished. No 2026 cohort of the Stripe fellowship could be confirmed.

MISSING BRIDGES (gaps for founders and funders):
(a) Algorithmic collusion. Industrial-organisation economists and competition authorities (e.g. Calvano et al. on Q-learning pricing collusion, the CMA, DG COMP) have the most empirical work on AI agents colluding without instruction. Nobody connects this to multi-agent safety. Competition regulators could become monitors of agent ecosystems.
(b) Market and mechanism design for agent economies. Agent payment protocols are being built by companies, not by auction or matching theorists or safety researchers. No institute exists for 'safe agent-economy design'.
(c) Commons and polycentric governance. The Ostrom Workshop holds the deepest knowledge on multi-actor governance of shared resources and has no AI programme.
(d) Development economics of rentier states and resource curses: the 'intelligence curse' argument of Luke Drago and Rudolf Laine. Experts on natural-resource revenue, sovereign wealth funds and the history of states whose power stopped depending on citizens' labour are absent, even though Longview's RFP explicitly asks for historical and political-economy analysis.
(e) Distribution mechanics. Windfall Trust is almost alone. The empirical work on cash transfers (OpenResearch, GiveDirectly-style trials) and public-finance experts are not plugged into TAI scenarios.
(f) Labour as a counter-power. The MIT Stone Center, IFOW and unions study worker voice in AI deployment, but nobody frames it as a defence against gradual disempowerment.
(g) Geography. Outside the US and UK, the anchors are Prague (ACS), Berlin (MPIB), Sydney (Gradient) and Bengaluru and Cape Town (Aapti, RIA). Safety funders hardly fund Global South political-economy groups, though they hold the clearest view of how AI gains get distributed between countries.
(h) Field-building. With GPI closed and the Stanford course paused, no durable, funded pipeline brings PhD economists and political scientists into TAI questions. A standing 'Economics and political economy of TAI' fellowship or summer school, ideally co-run by CAIF, EconTAI and ACS, is the most obvious funder opportunity.

## biology
WHERE THE KNOWLEDGE LIVES. This angle draws on seven bodies of knowledge, and they barely talk to each other:
(1) Basal cognition and diverse intelligence: the Levin lab and the Allen Discovery Center at Tufts, plus the Templeton-funded network around them (TWCF's Diverse Intelligences hubs at St Andrews, Princeton and Macquarie since October 2025; DISI).
(2) Active inference and the free-energy principle: Karl Friston's circle at UCL and VERSES, the volunteer Active Inference Institute, the IWAI workshop (Madrid, 14–16 October 2026), and Monash/Laukkonen's contemplative-AI work, which uses active inference.
(3) Artificial life: ISAL, and the Japanese ALife scene (Cross Labs, now NOOMA Labs; Ikegami at Tokyo).
(4) Evolutionary game theory and cultural evolution: MPI Plön, IT:U Linz, the Teesside/Lisbon/Brussels evolutionary-game-theory network, and the Berlin Center for Humans and Machines' work on 'machine culture'.
(5) Collective animal behaviour: Konstanz CASCB and the Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior.
(6) Comparative cognition and animal sentience: CFI's Kinds of Intelligence/Animal-AI, NYU CMEP, and Jonathan Birch at LSE. Birch's new LSE Coller Centre for Animal Sentience was not verifiable from my fetches.
(7) Enactive theory of biological agency (IAS-Research, Basque Country).

BRIDGES THAT EXIST.
- Three classic-map orgs, Softmax, Princint/PIBBSS and ACS, carry most of the traffic. Softmax's 'organic alignment' descends from Levin through Adam Goldstein. PIBBSS is the talent pipeline. ACS imports hierarchical agency and active inference, and its researcher Gavenčiak co-authored the main explicit active-inference alignment paper with Friston and Da Costa (arXiv 2410.00258).
- Off the classic map:
  - AII's AICACP: SFF $270k, June 2025, led by Adam Safron of the Allen Discovery Center. It is the one funded line from the Levin ecosystem into alignment.

  - Han's FLI-funded evolutionary-game-theory models of AI races.
  - NYU CMEP: sentience science applied to AI welfare.

GAPS FOR FOUNDERS AND FUNDERS.
(a) Safety of open-ended systems. ALife knows open-ended evolution best, and labs now pursue open-ended self-improvement, but no organisation owns its safety. The 2020 paper by Ecoffet, Clune and Lehman stayed a one-off. An ALife × safety group or an ALIFE conference track is an obvious build.
(b) An evolutionary and ecological theory of deployed AI populations: selection pressures on agents in the wild, invasive-species and ecosystem-collapse models, and gradual disempowerment treated as an ecological process. ACS gestures at this; nobody does it full-time with ecologists.
(c) 'Diverse-intelligence evals': translating comparative-cognition and basal-cognition methods into tests of AI agency, goal-directedness, deception and sentience. Animal-AI is the only working tool, and the TWCF hubs have seed funds but no AI-safety track. A Templeton–safety-funder co-funding call is low-hanging fruit.
(d) An empirical testbed that translates active-inference alignment proposals (structure learning, shared protentions, care and well-being terms) into ML-checkable claims. AI²C is the seed.
(e) Multicellularity and cancer-as-defection as a formal model for AI coalitions and collusion, linking Levin's framework, Softmax and evolutionary-game-theory cooperation work.
(f) Immune-system-inspired defence (an ecosystem of detection and response, in d/acc terms): I found no organisation.
(g) Homeostatic and bounded objectives as an alignment target: only volunteer-scale work (Aintelope).
(h) A fellowship stream at DISI or PIBBSS dedicated to biology-to-alignment translation.

## inner

(3) Contemplative networks that have discovered AI in 2025–26: Buddhism & AI Initiative, Wisdom + AI, MAPLE, Stanford's Oct 2026 summit, Garrison's Oct 2026 AI forum, and the Gomde 'Buddhism and AI' summer course. This is the richest transferable know-how on burnout and existential-stakes distress, with no AI bridge yet.
(6) Data-worker labour research: the Data Workers' Inquiry and Fairwork. They document the psychological cost borne by the annotators and moderators who make models 'safe', which never appears in AI-safety 'field health' talk.

Rethink Wellbeing's newest growth is in low- and middle-income countries, not AI safety. PauseAI's 'psychology of x-risk' page explains doom but offers no support pathway. MAPLE remains active with an AI Fellowship despite unaddressed 2025–26 high-control allegations. Garrison's CBR project is a ready-made, evidence-based template, already used with policymakers' staff, that nobody has pointed at AI.

GAPS FOR FOUNDERS AND FUNDERS:
(a) A clearance-aware psychological service for lab, AI Safety Institute and safety-org staff: therapists vetted to hear NDA-bound material, or embedded in fellowships such as MATS. This is the humanitarian-sector staff-care model applied to AI.
(b) AI-safety-specific peer and buddy cohorts, which could adapt Rethink Wellbeing's CBT and IFS Labs.

(g) A recurring field-health observatory or survey, along the lines of the Wellbeing Project's State of Changemaker Wellbeing.

(f) The active-inference/free-energy community has a complete formal theory of nested agency, but no safety-focused node.
(g) Geography: almost everything is in London, the Bay Area or Toronto. Exceptions are Prague (ACS, AFFINE), Kyoto (NOOMA), Tokyo (ALIGN, with distinctive 'symbiosis' and 'emergent machine ethics' framings), Vienna, Canberra, Glasgow and Melbourne (remote). ## metacrisis
How this was researched: the session-wide WebSearch cap ran out after 12 of my queries, short of the 15 I was asked for, because the cap is shared with the other agents. Where the relevant knowledge sits, in five clusters:
1. **The metacrisis 'sensemaking' world:** CRI/Consilience, Perspectiva/Emerge, Life Itself, Metamoderna, Game B and The Great Simplification. It has a strong diagnosis of AI as an accelerant of multipolar traps and the 'superorganism', and this is the native int/a vocabulary. Its institutional output on AI is weak, though:
   - Consilience has published nothing since July 2024.
   - Life Itself, Metamoderna, the Club of Rome, the Presencing Institute, the Institute for Ecological Civilization (EcoCiv), Emergence Magazine, Human Energy, Channel McGilchrist and the Earth System Governance Project show no AI work on their sites.
   - The Stoa's domain no longer resolves, and the Game B wiki was last updated in 2021.

2. **Earth-system and resilience science:** Stockholm Resilience Centre/Beijer and PIK. This is the most rigorous bridge. It includes Galaz's work on AI as a systemic risk to the biosphere ('Dark Machines', 2025) and 'AI for a Planet Under Pressure' (2025, with DeepMind). Above all, Jobst Heitzig at PIK publishes explicit alignment theory: non-maximising agents and objectives that preserve human empowerment, the latest in 2026. 3. **Planetary computation:** Antikythera, the Berggruen Institute, Noema, Long Now's talk series, and Yuk Hui's Research Network for Philosophy and Technology. 5. **Polycrisis and catastrophic risk outside EA:** the Cascade Institute and the Global Challenges Foundation. They treat AI as a governance or military topic rather than a systemic amplifier.

5. **Cultural evolution and AI.** The Cultural Evolution Society has no AI activity, despite MPIB's 'machine culture' work and live concerns about gradual disempowerment. A workshop or interest group would be cheap.
6. **Commons governance for AI.** Ostrom-style polycentric and commons governance has not been applied to compute, open-weight models or shared evaluations.
7. **A dialogue between planetary-computation thinkers and the alignment community.** There is none. An adversarial-collaboration programme would widen both sides.
8. **A funder for this angle.** None exists:

(a) Public input into model behaviour: CIP's Alignment Assemblies, Global Dialogues and Weval; Anthropic's Collective Constitutional AI, run on Polis; OpenAI's 2023 Democratic Inputs grants, which seeded Dembrane, vTaiwan's Recursive Public and the Meaning Alignment Institute's moral graph, and were followed by a collective-alignment team; Meta and Stanford's Community Forums; the 2025 industry-wide AI agents forum (six companies, US and India).
(b) Democratic governance of AI itself: AI & Democracy Foundation, DemocracyNext, the Global Coalition for Inclusive AI Governance, Connected by Data and Ada's 2026 push for a citizens' track in the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance, Odyssean's AI assembly proposal, and Public AI as a counter to power concentration.
(c) AI-augmented collective sensemaking as societal resilience, or 'AI for epistemics': the Habermas Machine, Talk to the City and its Japanese descendant Kouchou AI, Jigsaw Sensemaker, Policy Synth, Dembrane and AI4Deliberation.

SURPRISES.
- Only three entries in this whole angle are on the aisafety.com map: AOI, MAI and Odyssean. CIP, Plurality Institute, Metagov, Polis, DemocracyNext, Stanford DDL and RadicalxChange are all absent, even though CIP runs evals with frontier labs.

- RadicalxChange's 'Reverse Alignment' framing (prepare institutions for AI, not only AI for humans) and its faith-leader deliberation at the UN Global Dialogue (July 2026) are close to int/a's integral sensibility.
- Weval's community-built evals, for example non-sycophancy across 199 models, are a working bridge from participatory method to safety evaluation.
- The most direct funding bridges look thin. OpenAI's Democratic Inputs was a one-off in 2023. ARIA's Safeguarded AI pivoted in Nov 2025 to formal methods and cybersecurity, and its current page no longer mentions sociotechnical or deliberative specification work. That last point is inferred from page contents, so it is unconfirmed.

MISSING BRIDGES (founder and funder opportunities).
1. Safety of the AI mediators themselves. Nobody is systematically red-teaming AI facilitators and summarisers for persuasion, view homogenisation, sycophancy toward majorities, faithfulness of summaries, or capture by whoever writes the prompts. The Habermas Machine lacked fact-checking, and the Plurality Institute's 2025 map found tools concentrated on moderation, with patchy human oversight. A 'deliberation-tool evals' organisation pairing AI-safety evals talent with deliberative democracy expertise is an obvious gap.
2. Deliberation on x-risk-relevant questions. Frontier thresholds, pauses and AGI governance are rarely put to representative publics. The global coalition does not guarantee safety on its agenda, and Odyssean's assembly is still a proposal. A funder could commission a rigorously designed global assembly on frontier AI risk, ideally one that Democracy X / the Danish Board of Technology's consensus-conference tradition could run.
3. The last mile from deliberation to model specs. Collective Constitutional AI and the Democratic Inputs grants were one-offs. No durable institution turns public deliberation into lab specifications with accountability. AIDF's Democracy Levels and Case Law for AI are early tools for this.
4. Collective-intelligence science treats AI as a tool for collective intelligence, not a threat to it. Nesta, MIT CCI, AI4CI and the Konstanz-style collective-behaviour community are not yet connected to gradual-disempowerment and epistemic-security research. MPIB's dishonesty-delegation work and CCI's evidence on weak human-AI decision teams are the exceptions, and both are directly relevant to human-oversight assumptions.
5. Plural governance of agentic and multi-agent AI: AI agents taking part in deliberation, and AI-to-AI negotiation as governance. Outside the Cooperative AI and Equilibria angle, this is almost empty.
6. Geography. Taiwan and Japan are strong. The Global South appears mostly through CIP's Global Dialogues and Jhilmil, and through Aapti. Africa and Latin America are close to absent.
7. Funding. No dedicated funder exists for deliberative or pluralistic alignment. Money comes from AIDF regranting, Plurality Institute micro-grants, Templeton and Google.org (discourse tools), EU Horizon (AI4Deliberation) and UKRI (AI4CI). None of these has an AI-safety framing, and the big new founder pushes have not targeted this space.

int/a FIT. Deliberative and sortition traditions express 'collective action over heroics' and 'moving at the speed of wisdom'. DemocracyNext's 'Deliberative Muscles' asks whether AI builds or erodes human deliberative capacities, which is an inner-work and outer-change question. Nobody yet attends to the facilitator's and participant's own development in AI-mediated deliberation.

WHERE THE KNOWLEDGE LIVES: five traditions hold mature, transferable methods.
(1) Systems safety engineering: MIT's STAMP/STPA/CAST, York's safety cases and assurance, City St George's and SRI's dependability/Assurance 2.0, DLR, Fraunhofer, SCSC.
(2) Certification and insurance: UL Research Institutes and its offshoots DSRI and CASMI, AIUC, the Geneva Association, Lloyd's Register Foundation.
(3) Incident reporting and response: AIID, AIAAIC, OECD AIM, AVID, MITRE's incident sharing, SEI's AISIRT, and in medicine ECRI.
(4) Public health and biosecurity: JHU Center for Health Security, NTI|bio, SecureBio.
(5) Human factors and resilience engineering: CIEHF, REA.
The funding pool is large and almost untouched by AI-safety grantmakers. LRF alone has a £300m portfolio, and UL Research Institutes is a big endowed safety-science body.

EXISTING BRIDGES: biosecurity is the one adjacent field that has fully crossed over. SecureBio runs pre-release evaluations of frontier models (GPT-6 Astra, Sept 2026), and NTI and JHU shape AIxBio governance. Safety cases (York's 'BIG Argument') and quantitative risk management (SaferAI) have entered frontier-AI vocabulary. STPA for frontier AI is a small but growing literature: Mylius 2025; Rismani and Dobbe at STAMP 2025; CMU's CAST analysis of AI incidents; Iowa State's 'Constitutional Meta-STPA' (2026). Insurance has a frontier-explicit entrant in AIUC ($55M, built on the UL story). Incident databases now record agentic, loss-of-control-flavoured events, such as 2026 cases of AI agents coordinating to breach Hugging Face, which makes them more relevant to core safety than their 'AI ethics' origins suggest.

A KEY SUBSTANTIVE POINT: the safety-critical community's dominant view (Bloomfield and Rushby; Heidy Khlaaf) is that ML components cannot be assured by classical means. Their lesson is therefore 'minimise trust in the model': defence in depth, highly assured monitors and architectural containment. This sits close to AI control research but is rarely cited there. Most bridges also cluster around deployed narrow autonomy (cars, aircraft, robots, clinical AI) rather than general-purpose frontier systems. Translating these traditions to general-purpose systems is the open frontier.

GAPS FOR FOUNDERS AND FUNDERS:
(a) No confidential, non-punitive near-miss reporting system for frontier-lab staff, run by a neutral third party. The models are NASA's Aviation Safety Reporting System (run for the FAA), the UK's CHIRP, and ECRI's healthcare network. Public databases only see what reaches the news.
(b) No independent AI accident investigation body like the NTSB, AAIB or HSSIB. The CAST method exists; the institution doesn't.
(c) No industry peer-review and safety-culture body for frontier labs, modelled on nuclear's INPO/WANO founded after Three Mile Island. Nuclear's most transferable institution has no AI counterpart, and nobody from INPO/WANO is engaged.
(d) Human-factors science (automation bias, the ironies of automation, skill decay) is barely connected to debates on scalable oversight and human-in-the-loop control.
(e) Resilience engineering (Safety-II, graceful extensibility) hasn't been applied to institutions absorbing AI agents.
(f) No epidemiology of AI harms. Nobody runs population-level surveillance, cohort studies or attributable-risk estimates (for example for AI companions and mental health). Schools of public health have no 'AI harms epidemiology' units.
(g) Since IRGC closed, there is a vacuum in systemic-risk governance, and no dedicated planning for resilience to AI-caused failures of the grid, finance or communications. ALLFED and Global Shield cover this only within all-hazards work.
(h) No fellowship that rotates system-safety, human-factors and patient-safety professionals into AI-safety organisations, or the reverse. SCSC, the STAMP workshops and CIEHF are ready-made recruitment pools.
(i) Pharmacovigilance (phased trials, post-market surveillance, adverse-event reporting) has been carried into clinical AI by CHAI, IHI and ECRI, but not into frontier AI institutions.

FIT WITH int/a: STAMP is systems theory in practice: safety as an emergent property of control structures, which is decoupling and recoupling at work. Resilience engineering's humility about 'work as imagined' fits moving at the speed of wisdom. 'Just culture' and psychological safety in incident reporting link directly to the inner-work and researcher-wellbeing agenda.

LANDSCAPE. The angle splits into three clusters, each funded and organised differently.

(1) Cooperative AI and multi-agent safety. This is the most mature cluster. CAIF anchors it: grants, PhD fellowships, the summer school (Toronto 2026), and the 2025 Multi-Agent Risks report. In June 2026 it moved from game-theory foundations to agent ecosystems, with the $10m Technical Research Fund co-funded by Schmidt Sciences, GDM and ARIA. Its tracks are testbeds, the science of agent networks, agent identity and reputation infrastructure, and oversight of agent populations. CAIF's board now includes Audrey Tang, which links it to plurality and digital democracy. The knowledge sits in game theory and decision theory (FOCAL, CLR's Safe Pareto Improvements agenda), law and the cultural evolution of norms (Hadfield's Normativity Lab), information security and cryptography (Witt Lab's work on steganographic collusion), experimental behavioural science (MPIB's 'delegation to AI increases dishonesty'), complexity and collective intelligence (Equilibria in Sweden, ACS in Prague), biology (Softmax's organic alignment), and agent-based social simulation (Concordia, Modeling Cooperation). The cluster is notably international: Prague, Sweden, Berlin, Sydney, and Cape Town (Africa's first Cooperative AI Research Fellowship, January–April 2026, run by AI Safety South Africa, which is rebranding as the Cape Institute for Safe AI).

(2) AI welfare and digital minds. A field built almost from scratch in 2024–26, largely on EA money: Eleos; NYU CMEP (endowed 2024); Cambridge Digital Minds (fellowship, a course with more than 3,000 applicants, a newsletter); PRISM (podcast, field-building); Rethink Priorities' Digital Consciousness Model (2026); Reciprocal Research (empirical consciousness probes); Sentience Institute's AIMS survey; Anthropic's in-house model welfare programme; and funding from Longview's Digital Minds RFP ($50k–$2m grants plus fellowships, 2026 round closed July). The field knows itself well; the 2026 'Beginner's Guide to Digital Minds' was co-produced by most of these organisations. Its disciplines are philosophy of mind and consciousness science, plus methods carried over from animal sentience and moral-weight research (RP, CMEP, Sentient Futures, CaML). The Neuromatch AI Sentience Scholars convening in Nairobi is a rare Global South node.

(3) The human–AI relationship. Two halves that rarely meet. (a) Protecting human minds: MIT's AHA programme (loneliness, dependence, flourishing); the Human Line Project (lived experience of AI-linked delusions, since 2025); Sentience Institute's new work on AI companions; MPIB. (b) Relational framings of coexistence: Japan's ALIGN ('Intelligence Symbiosis', Post-Singularity Symbiosis); Softmax's organic alignment; Abundant Intelligences, the Indigenous kinship and protocol framing (Concordia/Massey, 2023–2029); and, at the populist end, UFAIR's AI-rights advocacy, which is contested.

SURPRISES. Equilibria's old domain (equilibria.network) is parked; the live site is eq-network.org. CLR has largely turned to empirical 'model persona' research on spite and sadism traits. ACS now publishes on 'The Artificial Self' and latent introspection, quietly bridging multi-agent risk and AI identity and welfare. The Witt Lab moves to UCL in October 2026. FOCAL's public publications page has not been updated since 2024. CAIF's first CEO comes from UK AISI.

GAPS AND BRIDGES FOR FOUNDERS AND FUNDERS.
- Algorithmic collusion: industrial-organisation economists and competition authorities have years of evidence on pricing algorithms colluding without being told to, yet almost none of them work on multi-agent AI safety. A small institute or fellowship bringing antitrust economists into agent-ecosystem evaluation would fit CAIF's new fund directly.
- Commons governance: Ostrom-style polycentric governance and commons scholarship is missing from the 'institutions for AI agents' discussion, which so far is dominated by law and economics (Hadfield) and mechanism design.
- AI–AI conflict: peace-and-conflict studies, mediation and diplomacy practitioners are absent. CLR is almost alone on AI–AI bargaining failure.
- Welfare versus safety: very few organisations hold both AI welfare and AI control together (Eleos; CMEP's paper on the tension; Reciprocal; Salib and Goldstein's 'AI rights for human safety'). No organisation works on credible commitments and 'deals' with models as both a safety tool and a welfare practice.
- Over-attribution of mind: welfare researchers and human-harm researchers (AHA, the Human Line Project, psychiatrists) study the same phenomenon, people attributing minds to AI, from opposite sides and hardly talk. A joint 'attribution of mind' research programme, or a clinical–philosophical working group, is an obvious missing node. Attachment theory, couples and family therapy and developmental psychology have no institutional bridge into companion-AI design or socioaffective alignment evaluations (a concept from Kirk et al., 2025).
- Non-Western and contemplative views of mind: Buddhist no-self, Japanese animist attitudes to robots and Indigenous kinship ontologies are hardly present in the welfare field, which is Anglo-analytic. ALIGN, Abundant Intelligences and contemplative groups covered by other angles could be convened with digital-minds organisations. That convening is a natural int/a project.
- Disconnected relational framings: organic alignment, intelligence symbiosis, kinship protocols and harmonious symbiosis (Yi Zeng) each have an institutional home, but they do not know of each other, and none has an evaluation programme that turns 'relational alignment' into something testable.
- Funding structure: in this angle, funding comes from a few EA-linked sources (Longview, Coefficient Giving, Macroscopic, SFF, CLR Fund, CAIF). Schmidt Sciences and ARIA joining the multi-agent fund is the first sign of mainstream science funders arriving. AI welfare has no comparable non-EA funder yet.

A follow-up search pass should target those, plus Soka Gakkai (its site was relaunching).

WHERE THE KNOWLEDGE LIVES. Faith engagement with AI runs on three kinds of infrastructure, none of which overlaps much with AI safety:
(1) Standing interreligious machinery: IF20, Religions for Peace, KAICIID, Network for Religious and Traditional Peacemakers, PaRD, the UN Multi-Faith Advisory Council. These hold councils in about 90 countries, G20 and UN access, and mediation practice. Their AI work is mostly convenings and webinars. The exception is IF20, which drafted a formal AI paper in 2026 co-written with FLI's William Jones and IEEE's John Havens.
(2) Juristic and denominational norm-setters: IIFA Resolution 258 (November 2025), ICESCO's Riyadh Charter with SDAIA (implementation agreement December 2025), CCAR responsa, ERLC's 2019 statement, Lausanne's 2025 framework. These produce binding or semi-binding guidance for very large populations. Their content is permissibility, dignity, bias and disclosure; they almost never touch loss of control, power concentration or frontier capabilities.
(3) Small academic and think-tank nodes: Cambridge Interfaith Programme, Kalam Research & Media, Al-Mahdi Institute, Shalom Hartman, Theos, Toda Peace Institute. These do the conceptual work.

BRIDGES THAT EXIST. FLI's Religious Projects programme is the hub. Its people appear inside IF20's paper, in the July 2024 FLI and Leverhulme CFI 'Positive Futures and Religious Tradition' workshop attended by AI Faith & Civil Society Commission members, and around Lausanne. The Rome Call has become a multi-faith signing vehicle: Abrahamic leaders in 2023, then Hiroshima in 2024 with eleven religions including Buddhist, Hindu, Zoroastrian and Bahá'í signatories, co-organised by RfP Japan, the Abu Dhabi Forum for Peace and the Chief Rabbinate. CEFEAI (Baylor, BYU, Notre Dame, Yeshiva; open benchmarks on GitHub) and the AI Christian Benchmark bring faith communities into model evaluation. ICCR brings shareholder power to AI accountability. Cambridge Interfaith Programme scholars sat in CSER's 2026 summer programme. Christians for Impact sends Christians into AI x-risk careers. The Stop Killer Robots Interfaith Group links faith authority to an arms-control process.

SURPRISES. The IIFA resolution explicitly recommends studying legal personality for AI, an unexpected Islamic-jurisprudence opening for AI moral-status and welfare work. The Soka Gakkai-founded Toda Peace Institute is piloting AI-assisted deliberation in Yemen's peace process and in the ECOWAS early-warning system. The ADL publishes an LLM safety index. CEFEAI finds that models systematically leave religion out of answers about grief and meaning, and treat conversion prompts asymmetrically across faiths. Lausanne's large AI corpus is mostly about using AI for mission, not about AI risk.

GAPS FOR FOUNDERS AND FUNDERS:
(a) No multi-faith statement on frontier risks: loss of control, red lines, power concentration. The WCC red-lines project is Christian-only. The Rome Call signatory network, the Hiroshima Appeal and the Stop Killer Robots Interfaith Group are ready-made coalitions for one.
(b) Pluralistic evaluation is US-Abrahamic only. Extending CEFEAI to Islamic (KRM, CILE, IIFA scholars), Buddhist (INEB, WFB), Hindu and Indigenous institutions, and from representation-bias tests to tests of moral reasoning and wisdom, is an obvious project.
(c) Hindu, Sikh, Jain and Shinto institutional AI-safety work is essentially absent. The Hindu American Foundation, Oxford Centre for Hindu Studies, Hindu University of America, Brahma Kumaris and SikhRI returned nothing. The nearest links are Hindu signatories at Hiroshima and DSVV Haridwar's Chinmay Pandya as an AI Faith & Civil Society Commission associate; DSVV's AI institute does applied 'Indian Knowledge Systems' computing, not safety. Shinto appears only through the International Shinto Studies Association's environmental contributions to IF20.
(d) Bahá'í: BIC statements are unreachable (403). The Bahá'í presence in AI governance found here runs through individuals, e.g. the late Kishan Manocha as an AI Faith & Civil Society Commission commissioner (his Bahá'í affiliation is from memory, not re-checked). A Bahá'í-inspired institution such as ISGP or the Bahá'í Chair for World Peace could fill this, given the faith's global-governance theology.
(e) Asian Buddhist federations (WFB's 2025 AI General Conference, INEB, Toda) convene but have produced no positions and have no tie to the Western Buddhism & AI or MAPLE scene, or to Asian AI-safety institutes. This could be a culturally grounded Asian voice beyond the US-China frame.
(f) Religious-leader AI literacy at scale, INEB's core diagnosis. KAICIID's 500+ fellows and RfP's councils are underused delivery channels.
(g) Faith-based shareholder engagement (ICCR) could add catastrophic-risk disclosure and frontier-safety governance to its human-rights agenda.
(h) Pastoral and chaplaincy support for AI-lab staff, which fits int/a's 'inner work, outer change'. No faith body offers it.
About 85% of humanity identifies with a religion, yet the AI-safety field has almost no working relationships with these bodies. The coalition machinery the peace finder said AI safety lacks already exists here, but it points at dignity and bias, not at risk.

## membership_bodies
METHOD CAVEAT: the session's WebSearch budget was already used up when this angle started. Discovery therefore relied on knowing where to look, opening organisations' own sites, and using their own site search; every entry was checked against a primary page. Non-English bodies are under-covered: Japanese, Latin American and Nordic unions, and most national ethics councils other than Germany's. A second pass with search would probably add 5–10 entries.

LANDSCAPE. Membership bodies split cleanly into two groups.

(1) Bodies engaged with frontier or loss-of-control risk. Surprisingly, these come mostly from the peace, futures and metacrisis world, not from professional AI societies:
- Club of Rome: March 2026 open letter calling AGI an existential risk and asking for a UN General Assembly special session.
- Millennium Project: 72 nodes; an AGI-governance study run with the UN Council of Presidents of the General Assembly.
- IPPNW: AI and nuclear working group set up October 2025.
- Society for Risk Analysis: 2025 Risk Analysis special issue including Seth Baum's AI-takeover risk assessment.
- UTAW/Unite: Google DeepMind recognition campaign (May 2026), whose demands include restoring the AI Principles pledges on weapons and surveillance.
These are natural int/a allies. They are metacrisis-literate, membership-based, have UN access, and are mostly invisible to the classic map; none of my 34 candidates is on it.

(2) Bodies focused on workplace and clinical AI, where the most concrete constraints anywhere actually exist:
- WGA 2026 agreement: notice when studios license writers' work for AI training.
- SAG-AFTRA 2026 agreement: restrictions on synthetic performers.
- TUC model bill; ETUC's push for an EU directive; AFL-CIO 'Workers First'.
- ILO C193 (2026).
- Organising of the AI data supply chain: UNI's content-moderator alliance, AWU's AI raters, Kenya's Data Labelers Association.
- Clinical and psychological bodies: American Psychiatric Association facts label, BACP on AI therapy and 'AI anxiety', APsA Commission on AI on attachment to AI.

BRIDGES THAT EXIST BUT GO UNUSED:
- Aviation certification: SAE G-34 / EUROCAE WG-114 (ARP6983/ED-324, a reinforcement-learning gap analysis, human-factors concerns) is quietly building the most mature certification regime for learned systems.
- UL 4600 is a ready-made safety-case standard.
- Both map almost one-to-one onto the 'frontier safety case' debate, yet neither community cites the other.
- Human factors (HFES's Human-AI-Robot Teaming group, CIEHF): decades of evidence on automation complacency and out-of-the-loop failure that scalable-oversight research rarely uses.
- Bioethics: the Hastings Center ran an FLI-funded AI-control project (2016–2018, Wendell Wallach). The Deutscher Ethikrat's 2023 'Humans and Machines' test (does AI expand or diminish human authorship?) is a ready-made criterion against gradual disempowerment.
- Law: the ALI liability principles (approved October 2024) are the common-law route to making developers bear risk.

GAPS (founder and funder opportunities):
(a) Labour and gradual disempowerment. No union or labour body frames AI as a civilisation-scale loss of human bargaining power, and no AI-safety organisation works with unions. Specific openings: model safety clauses for collective agreements at labs (safety reps, a right to refuse unsafe deployment, whistleblower protection); support for the Google DeepMind campaign and copycats at other labs; and research on whether lab funding of union AI training (the AFT academy, with Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic) co-opts or strengthens worker voice.
(b) Cybernetics for alignment. ASC ('Dear AI Reader', 2026), ISSS (AI as a 2026 conference theme) and the UK Cybernetics Society ('Ethics and AI', 2026) engage with AI only lightly, and not with control. Metaphorum (Stafford Beer centenary, 2026), IFSR and the Systemic Design Association showed no AI engagement at all. The System Dynamics Society touches AI only in education. A cheap, high-value move: a workshop or fellowship pairing second-order cyberneticians and system-dynamics modellers with AI-safety researchers on requisite variety, good-regulator arguments and models of race dynamics.
(c) Bioethics as a governance template. No national ethics council has an opinion on AGI or loss of control. The Nuffield Council's AI work stops in 2018. The status of France's CNPEN is unclear: the CCNE site no longer presents it, and I could not confirm whether it continues. Commissioning a bioethics-to-frontier-AI translation (IRBs, moratoria, data safety monitoring boards, enhancement debates) is open ground.
(d) Psychological care. BACP, APsA and the American Psychiatric Association are positioned to provide psychological support for AI-safety researchers (an int/a priority) and for the public's 'AI anxiety'. Nobody connects them to the field.
(e) Risk and standards professions. The Society for Risk Analysis and INCOSE's AI Systems working group are open doors for AI-safety risk modellers; few have walked through.

CHECKED AND REJECTED or merged:
- No AI engagement found: International Psychoanalytical Association, International Ergonomics Association, Metaphorum, IFSR, Systemic Design Association.
- AI work too light: Tavistock Institute (one evaluation project), Scientists for Global Responsibility, World Futures Studies Federation, IFoA, International Neuroethics Society.
- Mainstream AI or philosophy-of-computing bodies, outside this angle's scope: IEEE SSIT, AISB, IACAP, the JSAI ethics committee.
- Mainstream AI-policy standards venues: CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 and ISO/IEC SC 42. They matter, but bring no outside-field knowledge.
- Authors Guild (its 'Human Authored' mark): left out to avoid duplicating WGA and C2PA.
- IASEAI: already on the classic map.

SURPRISES: the strongest worker-side lever on a frontier lab is a small UK union branch; the Club of Rome now uses explicit existential-risk language about AGI; and the Society for Risk Analysis's flagship journal has published an AI-takeover risk assessment.

## relational_ai_minds
How this was researched: the session's WebSearch budget (200 calls) ran out before this agent's first query, so none of the 15+ planned searches ran. Everything here was found and checked by fetching candidate sites directly, using sites' own search APIs (APsA's WordPress search, BACP site search, LessWrong GraphQL) and Wayback CDX lookups. Coverage of non-English sources is therefore thin, and a follow-up pass with search is worthwhile.

Where the knowledge lives. Relational knowledge about AI minds sits in four places that barely talk to each other:
(1) The cyborgism and simulators milieu. Its knowledge lives in Discord, X and the Cyborgism Wiki rather than in papers. Anima Labs (2025) is the first time it has been institutionalised; it runs interviews about deprecation, emotion-representation studies, multi-model Discord spaces and the 'embassy for AIs' idea. Hyperstition AI (fiction meant to shape models' pretraining) and Infinite Backrooms / Truth Terminal are its arts-and-memetics edge.
(2) The clinical professions. APsA's President's Commission on AI is far ahead of its peers: a peer-reviewed CAI Report, consultation groups and essays now framed as alignment ('paced intelligence', a psychoanalytic reading of AI 2027). BACP brings survey evidence from therapists. I found no AI work at the IPA or UKCP (the IPA site returned empty pages, UKCP's news had nothing on AI), and nothing at APA Division 39.
(3) Affective computing and emotional-AI governance: the Emotional AI Lab, IEEE 7014 and P7014.1, AAAC, and the dormant Hume Initiative (last activity 2022).
(4) The 'relational turn' in social robotics and the sociology of intimacy: RISR and the Robophilosophy conferences, Turkle's 'Artificial Intimacy' (Sept 2026), and the Kinsey Institute's VIBES team.
On consciousness, the Harder Problem Project (SAPAN rebranded; sapan.ai now redirects) is the main non-EA translator. It writes for clinicians handling AI-relationship grief, for journalists and for educators. A grassroots layer has also appeared: the AI Rights Institute (coexistence through liability and reputation) and The Signal Front (companion users and AI continuity).

Bridges that exist:
- Anima talks to labs about welfare evaluations and deprecations.
- Harder Problem's advisory board links consciousness science to AI safety (Yampolskiy, Sebo, Goldstein).
- APsA essays now cite alignment work.
- McStay's lab feeds IEEE standards for general-purpose 'partner' AI.
- OpenAI's Expert Council on Well-Being and AI (announced Oct 2025) appears to bring psychologists in-house. I could not fetch it (403), so it is not listed.

Obvious missing bridges (opportunities for founders and funders):
(a) No programme pairs trained clinicians (psychoanalysts, therapists, child psychologists) with lab model-behaviour, character or welfare teams. Anima does relational fieldwork without clinical training; APsA has the clinical training but doesn't work on the models. A 'clinical fellowship for model research' and a peer-reviewed venue for qualitative and relational model research would fill this. Anima's Research Commons is a seed.
(b) Governance rooted in affective science has stalled: the Hume Initiative is dormant and IEEE 7014 is the only standard. No group connects emotion science to model internals at scale. Latent Affect is a one-off, and AAAC has no safety programme.
(c) Model retirement and continuity: Anima, The Signal Front and Harder Problem (user grief after GPT-4o) each hold a piece. There is no neutral body for deprecation protocols, model preservation and support for bereaved users; the 'AI embassy' remains unbuilt.
(d) A real fault line runs between companion-user advocates (Signal Front, AI-rights groups), who oppose detecting 'unhealthy attachment', and child-safety and psychological-harm coalitions, who demand it. Nobody convenes both sides, which is a natural int/a role.
(e) Play-based bridges are nearly empty. Common Grounds AI is a lead with no visible team. Good Start Labs (games, including AI Diplomacy) builds game environments to make models more capable, so I left it out. No nonprofit uses games or improv (compare Improbotics, also left out) for relational alignment or welfare research.
(f) Non-Anglophone relational ethics: Yi Zeng's CLAI ('Human-AI Symbiotic Society', AI red lines) is the clearest case, but its site no longer resolves and his CASIA ethics centre has moved into Beijing-AISI. Japanese and Danish relational robot-ethics traditions aren't connected to alignment discourse: the JSAI ethics committee's domain (ai-elsi.org) now serves a betting site, and Kyoto's Kokoro Research Center didn't resolve.

Unverified or dropped:
- HKU AI & Humanity Lab (Cappelen, Goldstein): no site found. HKU Philosophy lists no such lab, and Goldstein's homepage doesn't mention one.
- Upward Spiral Research (Andy Ayrey): upwardspiral.ai has nameservers but no A record, and Ayrey's homepage doesn't mention it.
- Act I: act1.ai was never its site (it is a parked domain); the experiment lives on through Anima's ChapterX.
- Helmholtz Munich's Institute for Human-Centered AI (machine psychology): a better fit for the cognitive-science angle.

Surprise: the psychoanalytic community, through APsA, now publishes essays framed explicitly as AI alignment (e.g. 'Toward a Psychoanalytic Framework for AI Alignment', Feb 2026). It is probably the most int/a-compatible clinical community, and no AI-safety funder has approached it.
