## Who it's for: Integral Altruism (int/a)

Integral altruism (int/a) is a young movement (London hub, communities in Berlin and Paris; first summit July 2025) of EAs, EA-adjacents and post-EAs. Its claim: the EA toolkit is powerful but incomplete, and it should be integrated with wisdom, systems thinking, complexity science, metarationality, metamodernism/metacrisis thinking and inner development. Five principles:
1. Full-spectrum knowing: rigorous rational epistemics plus embodied, intuitive, ecological and experiential knowing.
2. Moving at the speed of wisdom: discernment about when to act and when to wait; collective action over heroics; avoid power-seeking (and avoid exacerbating the AI race).
3. Decoupling and recoupling: analyse problems in isolation, but also see their systemic interconnection (AI risk within the metacrisis).
4. Fractal altruism: scope-sensitive ambition alongside local, intrinsic values.
5. Inner work, outer change: psychological, emotional and spiritual development as a foundation for sustainable impact.
Substack: integralaltruism.substack.com. Site: integralaltruism.com.

## The brief (from the int/a Europe WhatsApp group, Oct 2026)

- One member: a good int/a project would be "mapping adjacent fields for existing or possible AI Safety work and making it legible for existing and new funders". There is much talk of founding more AI safety orgs (Coefficient Giving's Project Tailwind, a $200M call for founders), but little about *widening* the field. New funders are arriving and looking for approaches that haven't been tried.
- Another member: the aisafety.com team, updating their field map, had little sense of adjacent fields ("wasn't the focus"). That's a huge gap: blind spots, not knowing where other fields have existing approaches or knowledge, and the field becoming more myopic over time.
- The group asked for a concrete list of problems for founders and opportunities for funders.
- A member suggested that perhaps the int/a project is the mapping itself, as an interdisciplinary exercise bringing together people from many disciplines.
- Another member noted that Principles of Intelligence (Princint, formerly PIBBSS) brings people and ideas from other disciplines; ACS (Alignment of Complex Systems) has an interdisciplinary/complexity-theory orientation; people using the label "cooperative AI" are often int/a-flavoured.

## Scope: what counts

An entry is an organisation or initiative (nonprofit, academic lab or centre, research programme, fellowship, funder or funding programme, network/coalition/community, startup, publication or media project) that works on making the transition to advanced AI go well (alignment, safety, governance, societal resilience, protecting human minds and relationships, AI welfare, civilisational design, the health of the people doing the work) AND brings knowledge, methods or perspectives from outside the mainstream ML-alignment and conventional AI-policy canon: other disciplines (biology, complexity science, physics, neuroscience, cognitive science, psychology, contemplative traditions, philosophy, theology, ecology, economics, political theory, law, anthropology, STS, arts), systems/metacrisis framings, inner development, pluralism and collective intelligence, care ethics, Indigenous and non-Western knowledge.

Four rings of proximity to the AI-safety core:
- core: AI-safety/x-risk organisations (often already on the aisafety.com map) with an interdisciplinary or integral bent.
- bridge: rooted in another field or tradition, but explicitly working on AI safety, x-risk or "AI going well".
- adjacent: working on AI's human or societal effects from another tradition, without an x-risk framing.
- potential: organisations in adjacent fields holding highly relevant knowledge or methods, which have begun (even lightly) to engage with AI, or plainly should.

Exclude: pure capabilities companies, generic "AI for good" applications (AI for climate, health, etc.) unless the work is about making AI itself go well, compliance consultancies, individual blogs/people (note notable individuals separately, but the unit is the organisation).
