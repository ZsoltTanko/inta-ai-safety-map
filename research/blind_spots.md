# Blind-spot sweep: what was searched and found (Oct 2026)

- [library] Search method (read first): thin. This session's WebSearch budget (200 calls) was already used up before this sweep: all 5 queries I tried were refused, so the 15-query minimum was not met. Candidates came from prior knowledge, checked one by one with about 60 direct WebFetch reads of each organisation's own site. Expect misses among organisations I didn't already know of, especially outside English. A sweep with a fresh search budget is worth running.
- [library] Library federations and associations (IFLA, ARL/CNI, ALA, CILIP, LIBER, EBLIDA, Deutscher Bibliotheksverband): found. IFLA (with its KR21 programme) and ARL/CNI have standing AI work. The ALA, CILIP, LIBER and dbv home pages showed only incidental AI items (ALA learning resources; LIBER TDM and copyright focus areas). ifla.org and cni.org blocked fetching (403), so IFLA is confirmed through KR21.
- [library] Archives and archival science (ICA, SAA, Digital Preservation Coalition, IIPC, InterPARES): thin. InterPARES Trust AI (2021-2026) is the one substantial node: archival diplomatics applied to AI and provenance. ICA showed no AI work; SAA has only a publishing AI statement; DPC and IIPC have only toolkit notes and conference posters. That's a real gap: professional archival bodies are barely engaged.
- [library] Web and software archives (Internet Archive, Software Heritage, Common Crawl): found. Software Heritage's CodeCommons is a real programme on traceable, opt-out-respecting code training data. The Internet Archive's AI engagement is light (essays, copyright comments). Common Crawl is AI data infrastructure with an opt-out registry, included as potential with a caveat.
- [library] Dataset provenance and documentation (Data Provenance Initiative, Data Nutrition Project, data provenance standards): found. DPI and the Data Nutrition Project are included. DPI's 2025-26 activity is unclear (repo last updated March 2025). The Data & Trusted AI Alliance (formerly Data & Trust Alliance), with 2026 data provenance standards for AI agents, is excluded as a corporate consortium (Meta, IBM, Walmart, etc.) with no outside-tradition lens.
- [library] Knowledge commons, open licensing and copyright for AI (Creative Commons, Open Future, COMMUNIA, Authors Alliance, Library Futures): found. A dense, active European and US cluster making the 'AI and the commons' and public AI argument; Open Future is the strongest. Funding links: Mellon funds the library corpus projects, Arcadia funds KR21; neither has its own AI programme.
- [library] Training data stewarded by libraries and public-interest corpora (IDI, Public Interest Corpus, Transparent Books, national libraries): found. A new and growing model: libraries as accountable stewards of training data. Includes Harvard's IDI, Transparent Books (Mellon, 2026-2031) and Open Future's European library books feasibility study. Among national libraries, only the KB's position was confirmed; the National Library of Norway's AI-Lab site is live, but its home page made no claim about its copyright-value study.
- [library] Content provenance and integrity of digital records (Starling Lab, WITNESS, Mnemonic, Content Authenticity Initiative): found. Starling Lab and WITNESS have sustained AI and deepfake programmes grounded in archival and evidentiary practice. C2PA is already on the roster. The Adobe-led Content Authenticity Initiative was left out as a corporate programme overlapping C2PA.
- [library] Information schools and information-integrity research (UW CIP, OII, Berkeley CLTC, IPIE, Forum on Information & Democracy, ASIS&T, iSchools): found. Several centres with sustained AI work are missing from the roster: CIP, OII, IPIE, FID, and Berkeley's AI Security Initiative (CLTC is on the aisafety.com map). ASIS&T showed only one generative-AI workshop; the iSchools consortium wasn't checked.
- [library] Research data stewardship (RDA, CODATA, GO FAIR, ODI): thin. RDA has small standing groups on generative-AI documentation and access for AI agents; the ODI runs a data-centric AI series. CODATA and GO FAIR weren't checked.
- [library] Communities for AI in libraries, archives and museums (AI4LAM, Europeana, LC Labs): thin. AI4LAM is active; its provenance metadata for AI transcripts is relevant, but most of its work is AI adoption, not AI governance. Europeana blocked fetching. LC Labs' AI experiments concluded in 2024, with no 2025-26 AI programme seen.
- [library] Non-English sweep (German, Dutch, Norwegian, French library bodies): thin. Only direct site checks were possible: the dbv showed no AI work on its home page, the Dutch KB has an AI statement, and the Norwegian national library's home page showed nothing on AI. No French or Spanish bodies were checked. This is the weakest part of the sweep.
- [library] Taxonomy fit: thin. The taxonomy has no library and information science key. Homes were assigned as follows: provenance and knowledge-access bodies to democracy (dem_epistemics), data and knowledge commons to economics (econ_commons), copyright advocacy to law. If this cluster is kept, a library and information-science sub-tradition or seam may be worth adding.
- [education] AI literacy and AI-risk education for publics and schools: found. School AI-literacy work is a large, active field (Day of AI, Experience AI, TeachAI, AILit/PISA 2029, UNESCO education, Digital Promise, EDSAFE, News Literacy Project, Elements of AI, KI-Campus). Almost all of it is framed around present harms and readiness, not catastrophic risk. Education about AI risk for the public is thin: AISAP, Leaf, the CAIS course, and Seismic (already on the roster). Checked and dropped: AI4K12 (no activity after 2024), Non-Trivial (not AI-focused), EdTech Hub (deploys AI tools). CivAI, Council of Europe education and IFLA's AI group returned 403 and are unverified.
- [education] Teachers and their unions on AI: found. Unions engage substantively: AFT (a training academy with OpenAI, Microsoft and Anthropic), NEA (task force principles, AI hub), Education International (critique of AI literacy frameworks as data extraction), ETUCE (2026 campaign against training AI on student data), and the Chartered College of Teaching (government-funded AI course). NEU, NASUWT, GEW and UCU pages were blocked (403/404), so European national unions are under-sampled. That is a search artefact, not evidence that they are absent.
- [education] Learning scientists studying cognitive offloading and deskilling: thin. No dedicated centre for cognitive offloading or deskilling by AI was found. The best-known empirical studies are single papers rather than standing programmes: MIT Media Lab's 'Your Brain on ChatGPT' (AHA, already on the roster), Microsoft/CMU (a company), Wharton/Bastani in PNAS, and Gerlich. Organised work: Brookings Task Force (risks outweigh benefits, Jan 2026), SLATE/AI LEARN Bergen, IWM Tübingen, Cognitive Resonance, Stanford SCALE, Deans for Impact. Monash CoLAM ('metacognitive laziness'), ISLS and EARLI could not be verified or showed no AI programme. This is an open niche for founders and funders.
- [education] University-level AI-safety curricula outside CS: thin. The only standing AI-safety curriculum built for non-CS audiences is CAIS's AI Safety, Ethics and Society course. Others teach AI ethics or literacy rather than safety: Harvard Embedded EthiCS, the AAC&U institute, metaLAB's AI Pedagogy Project, SLATE's human-AI master's courses. LCFI, Oxford Institute for Ethics in AI, UChicago XLab and Cosmos are already on the roster. EDUCAUSE and Jisc could not be fetched (403 / certificate error), so the higher-education IT side is unverified.
- [education] Method caveat: search coverage: thin. The session's WebSearch budget (200 calls) was already used up before this sweep started, so no new web searches ran. Candidates came from prior knowledge and were each checked on their own sites with WebFetch. Non-English coverage is limited to German (KI-Campus, Forum Bildung Digitalisierung, IWM; GEW blocked) and French and Spanish probes (La main à la pâte and Learning Planet Institute showed no concrete AI programme; Ceibal blocked). Thin cells, especially Majority-World and Asian education bodies, should be read as search artefacts.
- [epidemiology] Method caveat for this sweep: thin. This session's WebSearch budget was used up before the sweep began, so I could not run any web searches. Discovery used Europe PMC bibliographic queries (titles, authors, affiliations) plus direct fetches of organisations' own pages. Several domains returned 403 errors or rate limits (rand.org, mind.org.uk campaign page, rcpsych.ac.uk on the first try). I ran no non-English web searches. Thin or empty cells below may be search artefacts and are worth a proper web pass.
- [epidemiology] Epidemiologists and clinicians studying chatbot-associated harms: found. A real, fast-growing cluster in 2025-26: Aarhus (EHR screening of psychiatric records), KCL Psychosis Studies (Lancet Psychiatry 2026), BIDMC Digital Psychiatry (typology, harms review, MindBench with NAMI) and RAND Health Care (prevalence surveys, suicide-response evals). Individuals publishing without an organisational programme, to note separately: Marc Augustin (Protestant University of Applied Sciences Bochum, several AI-delusion papers with Østergaard and Torous), Hudon & Stip (Université de Montréal), Veena Kumari (Brunel).
- [epidemiology] Harm surveillance (public-health style monitoring of AI harms): found. Flinders' repeated cross-sectional safeguard audits, CCDH's persona-based tests and KFF's tracking of AI health information are the organisational examples. ECRI's AI incident reporting via its Problem Reporting Network is already on the roster. No public-health body runs syndromic or sentinel surveillance of AI-associated presentations; the Aarhus EHR study is the nearest prototype.
- [epidemiology] Population mental health and mental-health charities: thin. Mind's 12-month Commission into AI and mental health (launched July 2026) is the one clear organisational programme. Samaritans, Molly Rose Foundation, Mental Health Foundation, YoungMinds, Mental Health Europe, IASP, Orygen, Foundation for Social Connection, Boston Children's Digital Wellness Lab and the AAP Center of Excellence on Social Media and Youth Mental Health showed no AI work on their home or news pages. Royal College of Psychiatrists could not be checked.
- [epidemiology] Explicit 'public health approach' to AI: thin. The framing exists in papers but not yet as an organisational programme. Examples: BMJ 2026 'AI as a new commercial determinant of health' (Itai Bavli, Nadav Davidovitch), EJPH 2026 'public health governance of AI chatbots used by adolescents' (Alec Cali, Heidelberg Institute of Global Health; Hannah van Kolfschooten, Basel) and the Lancet 2026 comment on chatbot advertising from Deakin GLOBE. APHA lists no AI policy brief, and I found no Lancet Commission on AI and health harms. This is an open niche for founders and funders.
- [epidemiology] WHO: thin. WHO's AI work is AI for health systems: the ethics guidance on LMMs, GI-AI4H, WHO Europe readiness reports, and many AI-as-tool papers. The Commission on Social Connection names unhealthy digital technology use but not AI companions. TU Delft's Digital Ethics Centre is the WHO collaborating centre on AI-for-health governance. I found no WHO workstream on the population mental-health effects of consumer chatbots.
- [epidemiology] National public-health institutes: searched_none_found. A Europe PMC affiliation sweep covered RKI, RIVM, Santé publique France, UKHSA, Public Health Scotland, CDC, the Norwegian Institute of Public Health, THL, INSPQ and other 'Institute of Public Health' units for 2025-26 chatbot or generative-AI papers. It found only AI used as a tool (evidence synthesis, guidance chatbots) plus one governance paper from USI's Institute of Public Health. No national public-health institute was found treating AI as an exposure or harm. Web search was unavailable, so non-English institute reports may have been missed.
- [epidemiology] Toxicology-style and pharmacovigilance approaches: thin. The transfers I found: Vanderbilt's algorithmovigilance system (pharmacovigilance applied to deployed AI), Flinders' call for 'AI vigilance', and BIDMC's 'pharmakon' framing and simulation-based risk estimation. The Society for Risk Analysis is already on the roster. I found no toxicology society, exposure-science group or IARC-style hazard classification of AI. The 'AI chatbot dependence' literature is mostly psychometric scale-building, with a sceptical counterpoint from Billieux's behavioural-addiction group in Lausanne ('People are not becoming AIholic', 2025).
- [epidemiology] Health regulators and clinical safety bodies: found. The FDA Digital Health Advisory Committee's Nov 2025 meeting on generative-AI mental-health devices is the clearest regulator entry point. ECRI, CHAI and the IHI Lucian Leape Institute are already on the roster. I did not attempt MHRA, Australia's eSafety Commissioner or EU bodies, because they sit closer to a law and regulation sweep.
- [audit] Professional bodies and standards setters for audit and assurance of AI (ICAEW, IIA, AICPA, IAASB and peers): found. ISACA (AI audit credentials) and the IIA (AI Auditing Framework, Three Lines Model) have real programmes on assuring AI. ForHumanity explicitly copies the financial-audit model. The IAASB and ICAEW work mainly on AI used inside audits, so they are potential. Not included: AICPA & CIMA (AI page returned 404, unverified), PCAOB (403), CAQ and FRC (no AI item visible on site), IFAC (hub on accountants' own AI use), Accountancy Europe (one 2026 factsheet), ICAI (nothing found). None of these bodies yet treats frontier-AI or AI-lab assurance as a programme, though AI-safety governance already borrows the IIA's Three Lines Model.
- [audit] Actuarial work on AI risk (IFoA, SOA, IAA and peers): found. IFoA, the IAA's AI Task Force, the SOA Research Institute, CAS and Australia's Actuaries Institute all run standing AI lines. All are framed as model governance, ethics and fairness; I found no actuarial body working on catastrophic or long-horizon AI risk, which looks like a real gap given actuaries' long-tail and solvency methods. Left out: the Actuarial Association of Europe and DAV (AI working groups, but output mostly defends traditional actuarial models' status under the AI Act), the CRO Forum (2026 insurer AI-governance guidance, compliance-flavoured), and the Cambridge Centre for Risk Studies (no AI line visible on its site).
- [audit] Central-bank and financial-stability work on AI systemic risk (BIS, FSB, Bank of England and peers): found. This is the strongest part of the area: in 2026 the FSB Chair, the Bank of England's FSR and the BIS all explicitly framed frontier AI as a financial-stability threat, mainly via cyber. The ESRB's December 2025 report and LSE's Systemic Risk Centre supply systemic-risk theory. MAS and the FCA contribute supervisory assurance methods. Not included: the ECB (a single May 2024 FSR special feature), and the IMF and IOSCO (their sites returned 403, so their AI chapters and reports could not be checked).
- [audit] Insurance of AI systems (actuarial pricing of AI failure; extra angle): found. Armilla (Lloyd's coverholder) and Munich Re aiSure price AI model failure and pair cover with evaluation. AIUC and the Geneva Association are already on the map. Swiss Re and Lloyd's own AI risk publications were not checked.
- [audit] Investor stewardship and fiduciary finance on AI (extra angle): thin.
